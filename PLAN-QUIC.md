# Plan — QUIC transport

The steps of the QUIC transport's slice ([Workflow](AGENTS.md#workflow)), in order, open items only: remove a step when it is done. Each step ends with the check chain passing for the crates it touches, and a stop for review. The slice ends when the user plays it in the client. It is a proposal for review, with no place yet in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md)'s order.

The user plays a match on a server over QUIC: the client reaches the server by its address and the TLS key its listing names, and a client that pins another key ends at once, saying so. The design is [QUIC transport](docs/design/15-quic-transport.md); each step folds its rules into designs 02, 05 and 10 where they change. Step Q1 adds `quinn-proto`, `quinn-udp`, `rustls`, `polling` and `bytes` as direct dependencies of a new crate, `campfire-quic`, which `net` depends on, pinned to the versions the lock holds, `rustls` with no default features and with `ring` and `std`, `quinn-proto` with `rustls-ring` alone, as the user approved.

1. **Q1. The QUIC IO** (`quic`, `net`, the patched Lightyear; D1 to D5, D7 to D9): `[patch.crates-io]` at the fork's `campfire-quic` branch, whose raw server keeps a `RemoteId` its link holds, the change `raw-server-keep-remote-id` offers upstream; the `quic` crate, which no binary uses yet, and its row in design 02's Modules table: the socket over `quinn-udp`, each datagram in a buffer of its own that a DATAGRAM frame then keeps with no copy; the endpoint and its table of connections behind one mutex, on the server's entity or the client's; the endpoint's thread, which holds the socket, waits through `polling`, takes the mutex for one batch at a time, and is joined at the endpoint's drop; the server's link entity, spawned whole once a handshake completes, its `RemoteId` its own entity, and Lightyear's raw server keeping it; the close of a connection at its link entity's removal; the client's IO; the frame's two systems, which do no IO and wake the thread once a frame; the datagram queue that drops its oldest; no stream either way; `LINK_MIN_MTU` on every link from its spawn, `SimClient::spawn_client`'s and the in-process ones included, the MTU that follows `max_size`, and the close of a connection whose `max_size` falls below it; Retry and no `NEW_TOKEN`; the stateless reset key the endpoint is given; the keep-alive and the idle timeout; the TLS configs of a raw Ed25519 key and a verifier of one pinned key, with no session ticket; the close that drains. Check, on loopback, each test below 1 s: a handshake, and datagrams both ways; `LINK_MIN_MTU` equals `max_size` once the handshake completes, a datagram of that size arrives whole, and a reliable message of three fragments arrives whole through Lightyear; a client whose connection ids are 20 bytes is closed; each accepted connection, a second one from the same client too, follows a Retry; a peer that opens a stream is closed; two connections from one client socket hold two link entities and two `RemoteId`s; a handshake that fails, and a connection lost between its handshake and its frame, leave no entity; a server's endpoint refuses a client before its `LinkStart` and after its `Unlink`; a pinned key that is not the server's fails the handshake by `access_denied`, and a second connect checks the key again; an `Unlink` on either end, a despawn of a server's link, and the server's end each end the other's link in the same frames, not by the idle timeout; with a short idle timeout, a peer whose thread stops is lost by it, and a link whose app runs no frame for twice that timeout stays, and carries datagrams again once the frames go on; a frame that sends wakes the thread, which writes before its next deadline; an endpoint's drop ends its thread; a key's encodings equal RFC 8410's vectors.
2. **Q2. The binaries on QUIC** (`protocol`, `net`, `server`, `client`, `lan-check`; D2, D6, D8): `TlsKey` in place of `CertificateHash` in the pin, the client's arguments, `Listening` and the LAN check; the TLS key and the reset key derived from the server's key, and the `tls` file and its code gone; the server's socket bound before its app runs; the server's and the client's QUIC IO; WebTransport's plugins, their asserts and the rule of one plugin for each process gone; `wtransport` and Lightyear's `webtransport` feature gone; designs 02, 05 and 10. Check: a known server key gives the TLS key and the reset key that an independent HKDF computes, and two server keys give two TLS keys; a server whose port is in use exits with failure, naming the address; the LAN check passes; no crate of the tree is tokio or `aws-lc-rs`; the user plays a match on the server over QUIC.
3. **Q3. The link's end** (`quic`, `net`, `lan-check`; D10): `LinkEnd` from the IO, on the link's entity; `CloseLink` and its `AppCode`, and `net`'s `CloseCode` at each place that unlinks with text today; the closes the app's exit writes; `LinkLost` as an enum of its causes. Check: each loopback test of Q1 that ends a link asserts its `LinkEnd`, and the peer of a `CloseLink` reads its code; an app that exits ends its peers' links by `Stopped`; an endpoint given the reset key of one that stopped resets the old connection's next packet, and the peer ends by `Reset`; the LAN check's verdict refuses an impostor that ends by another cause; the client logs `WrongServerKey` against a wrong pin.
4. **Q4. The answer bound to the TLS session** (`protocol`, `quic`, `net`; D11): `ChannelBinding` from each end's exporter, in the server's spawn of a link and on the client's entity as it links, and the fixed one of in-process links; the answer over `"campfire/connect/v2" ‖ challenge ‖ binding`; design 05's step 3. Check: the two ends of a loopback link read the same binding, and two links read different ones; an answer signed over another link's binding is refused; the in-process scripted matches pass as before.
