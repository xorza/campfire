# Plan — next steps

Open items only, in order. Remove an item when it is done. Each step ends with the check chain passing for the crates it touches, and a stop for review. Stage context: [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md); design: [design/](design/).

1. **Static bodies apart from moving ones**: the grid of buckets sizes its cells by the widest body in the tick, so one wide structure, a core of 1.5 m now and up to 64 m by the data's limit, makes every cell wide, and in the worst case the search checks every pair again. As 0 A.D. does, the bodies of units that cannot walk go into a static index, built from the same data as the pathing grid and changed only when such a unit dies or spawns; the moving bodies go into the grid of buckets, its cells twice the widest walker's radius; each walker looks up the static bodies in the cells it covers. Two static bodies never part, so no pair of them is checked. The pathing grid uses the same index: when a static body dies or spawns, it marks again only the cells that body covers, from the static bodies that cover them too, and keeps a count of blockers a cell where a bit cannot tell what stays blocked; a change no longer builds every layer again in one tick. A grid with levels of cell size, for walkers of very different sizes, waits for a mode that needs it, the RTS proof of stage 4 first. Test: the contacts equal a check of every pair in scenes with a static body of 64 m among walkers of 0.35 m; a walker's cells stay 0.7 m wide there. The pathing grid after a tower dies equals one built again from the rest.
2. **Long route**: A* on the pathing grid for the unit's radius, eight neighbors with no corner cut, integer costs 10 and 14, ties by estimate then cell index; line-of-sight smoothing; the nearest reachable cell for a goal no route reaches; a limit of expanded cells a tick for all routes, served in stable-id order. Test: routes around a wall with a gap, through the gap and not across a blocked corner, to hand-counted costs; the same route on every run; a tick that meets the limit finishes the rest in the next.
3. **Units walk their routes**: a move order, a chase and a path walker walk a route to their goal, from waypoint to waypoint, and plan again when the grid changes; the client plans its own units' routes on the same grid. Test: a hero ordered behind a tower walks around it, not into it; the replay gives the same hash; a client predicts the route with no correction.
4. **Short route around units that block**: a walker whose way a standing unit blocks plans in a small window around itself, the standing units marked as blockers, and goes round; a walker that another walker keeps from moving for a few ticks marks that one too, so two that meet head on pass. It still yields as collision does. Test: a hero walks round a standing hero and a standing creep to its goal; two heroes that walk head on pass each other; the replay gives the same hash.
5. **Design and content**: the script API and the navigation design name what the release runs; the lane and 3v3 tests play with routes. Test: the workspace chain, and the LAN check.
6. **Vertical slice playtest, fourth round**: the same 1v1 on LAN, with routes and collision. Units that block each other and body block are new to the feel; write down whether the match reads and plays well. This closes stage 3, or names what is missing.
7. **Collision bench**: on a quiet machine, run `cargo bench -p campfire-capabilities --features bench --bench collision`: 1000 bodies, crowded into 40 m square and spread over 120 m square, contacts found and parted. Check that it fits the Collide stage's share of a tick at 30 Hz, and record the numbers beside the collision design. A timing probe on a busy machine gave 0.14 to 0.42 ms, against 2.1 to 2.5 ms for a check of every pair.
