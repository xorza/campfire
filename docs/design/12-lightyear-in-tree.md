# Lightyear in the tree

The player hosts a LAN match, plays a singleplayer match and replays both as before, but every link runs on Campfire's own network stack: the fork of Lightyear copied into `source/crates/network/`, built as Campfire's crates, over its own sealed UDP, with no QUIC and no git dependency on Lightyear. It builds on [Engine core](02-engine-core.md#networking), [Sessions](08-sessions.md), [Replication audiences](10-replication-audiences.md) and [QUIC transport](11-quic-transport.md), whose transport it replaces. The facts it stands on are in [Lightyear in the tree](../research/lightyear-in-tree.md).

## Decisions

### The code

- **The stack is Campfire's code, copied once.** The fork's crates are copied from its `main` at `98ec6345`, which holds `canonical-state-and-bounded-passes`, into `source/crates/network/`, each a member of the workspace; from then on they change only here, and the fork's repository keeps their history. Refused: a submodule or a git dependency, which every change of the seam crosses as two repositories; a git subtree, which brings Lightyear's 2,300 commits into Campfire's log.
- **Its origin stays beside it.** The copy keeps the fork's MIT and Apache-2.0 texts, Campfire's own licenses, and a `README.md` in `crates/network/` names Lightyear and its upstream as the origin of the code. Upstream's MIT text holds no copyright line to keep.
- **Each crate is a module of Campfire's.** Each takes a row of the crates table, a place in the layer test, the workspace's lints and `clippy.toml`, and its checks through `cargo drive`; the fork's `xtask` goes, its check of the crate `clippy.toml` files and its refusal of an engine crate under a problem crate becoming tests of `rules`. Refused: a nested workspace, with a lockfile, profiles and a driver root of its own, which the layer test does not read.
- **The stack keeps its layers.** A problem crate holds no Bevy crate, a glue crate binds one to the ECS, and the test stepper is test support, as the fork's layering decides; a stack crate depends on Campfire's base crates, never on `sim` or a capability. Refused: folding the glue into `net`, which compiles the engine into every protocol and codec test.
- **The stack's `unsafe` is where an outside API asks.** At `98ec6345` the stack holds `unsafe` only for Bevy's `SystemParam` impls of its synced timelines, `polling`'s registration of a socket with its poller, and a test's counting allocator, each block saying why it is sound; [Unsafe only where an outside API asks](02-engine-core.md#platform) names Bevy and `polling` beside Win32 and `libc`. Refused: an exception for speed, which the fork's typed systems no longer need, as they walk no erased archetype.
- **The stack's crates are `campfire-net-<name>`.** Each keeps the fork's name of its problem, after `net-`, so `net`, Campfire's game networking, and the stack read as one family, and no name of the stack takes a word Campfire's crates hold. Refused: `campfire-lightyear-<name>`, which names a project the code no longer follows; the fork's bare names, as `sim`, `session` and `common`, which Campfire's crates hold.
- **A word keeps Campfire's meaning.** The stack's network simulation is the network emulator, `net-emulator`, its `SimNetwork` and `SimIo` an `EmulatedNetwork` and an `EmulatedIo`, as Linux's netem, whose terms its `LinkModel` takes, names it, since `sim` is the match's; its session is the packet session, `net-packets`, its `Session` a `PacketSession`, since a session is a hosted match ([Sessions](08-sessions.md)); its `Tick` is `NetTick`, the timelines' count, since `Tick` is the match's and `net` already imports the stack's as `NetTick`; its `SessionBinding` is `ChannelBinding`, RFC 9266's word, which `protocol` already signs. Refused: the fork's words beside Campfire's, one word with two meanings in one workspace.

### Merges with the base crates

- **One number type.** `fixed` leaves: a tick span is a `Num`, the overstep a `Num` in `[0, 1)`, and a span that cannot be negative a checked newtype of one. A `Num` holds the `I32F32` and `U32F32` ranges of tick spans, and its step of 2⁻²⁴ tick, 1 ns at 60 Hz, is finer than the 2⁻¹⁶ the wire carries. The emulator's probabilities are `Num`s too, as a test's data gives them. Refused: `fixed` beside `Num`, two fixed-point types with two rounding rules.
- **One tick rate.** `campfire-common` holds the one `TickRate`, which the sim, the session's terms and the stack's timelines read: ticks over a whole number of seconds, so a paced rate, half of 21 Hz among them, stays exact, and a tick's length rounded to the nearest nanosecond, the fork's rule, half the error of the sim's rounding down. A match's rate stays whole hertz, as its terms give it. Refused: the fork's whole `NonZeroU16` hertz, which holds no half of an odd rate; a float rate, which is not exact; two types of one quantity.
- **The fork's `common` stays a crate of its own, `net-common`.** It holds what several of the stack's crates share, the arena of a writer's chunks, the buffer of ready items, the seeded randomness and the benches' policy, below every other crate of the stack. Refused: `common` merged into `net-core`, as `net-bytes` depends on it and `net-core` on `net-bytes`, so the merge makes a cycle of crates.
- **One home for each shared helper.** The stack's threads start through `campfire_store::Worker`; its port map's shared port is `campfire_store::SharedPort`, the platform layer's ([One promise on every OS](02-engine-core.md#platform)); its `ErrorReport` is `campfire-common`'s and its `SplitMix64` `campfire-math`'s, which leaves `internals` as the emulator and a seeded endpoint draw from it in production. The stack's `Arena` and `ReadyBuffer` stay its own, as no other crate splits buffers into views. Refused: a copy of each beside Campfire's, which drift apart.
- **One encoding of a message and a component.** The stack's registration of a message or a replicated component takes a serde type and encodes it through `campfire_codec::Binary`; its custom-encoding forms and its `postcard_utils` go, and with them `net`'s `WireCodec` and the 18 bans of `clippy.toml` on Lightyear's registration. Refused: the bans, a rule a type can hold ([Direction](../../AGENTS.md#direction)).
- **The stack's own headers keep their codec.** `ToBytes`, `Reader` and `Writer` stay a crate of the stack, `net-bytes`, for its packets' fields. Refused: moving them into `codec`, which gives the gateway of the serde formats a second problem, as the fork's layering refuses an ECS feature in its byte codec.

### The seam

- **The links run on the stack's UDP.** `quic` goes; a server and a client link through the stack's sealed UDP endpoints, and the stack's port map maps the server's port. This reverses [QUIC on `quinn-proto`](11-quic-transport.md#the-stack), whose refusal of Lightyear's UDP held for an IO with no encryption and no congestion control, which the stack's now has; QUIC under the stack would repeat its packet session's ACKs and RTT, which the fork's session design refuses. Its cost: a third-party client implements Campfire's own UDP protocol from [the protocol spec](05-protocol-spec.md), which then holds its byte layouts, where today any QUIC library connects. Everything that exists only for QUIC goes with it.
- **Campfire keeps what is its own at the endpoint.** The server's secret, from which the stack derives its raw key and its reset key, derives from the server's own key, as the TLS key does now ([A raw key derived from the server's key](11-quic-transport.md#the-servers-key)); the ALPN is `campfire`; the connect answer signs the link's `ChannelBinding` ([The answer binds to the TLS session](11-quic-transport.md#the-connect-answer)); the stack's admission holds at most the session's seats for each address ([Limits for each address, from the session's seats](11-quic-transport.md#connections)).
- **One in-process link.** A singleplayer client and its local server, and every harness match, link through the emulator on the OS clock with a perfect model, the stack's one in-process IO, in place of `CrossbeamIo` ([Singleplayer is a local server](08-sessions.md#decisions)).
- **The harness's time is Bevy's.** A harness app's time follows `TimeUpdateStrategy`, which the stack's `Frame` clock reads, and its links follow the stack's `LinkModel` in netem's terms; the harness's `DelayLine`, its own `LinkModel` and the pin of Lightyear's round trip go. Refused: a delay line of the harness's own, a second model of a link beside the stack's.
- **The pace sets the tick rate.** A local match's speed gives its client and its local server the match's rate times the speed through the stack's `SetTickRate`, which `Time<Fixed>`, the timelines and the sync all read. Refused: a relative speed of `Time<Virtual>`, which the stack's conversions of time to ticks would not see.
- **The server's frame stays strict.** The stack's systems run in the server's strict `Update` and `FixedUpdate`, on one thread, so an ambiguity among them fails the build, as a Campfire system's does ([The server's frame declares its order](02-engine-core.md#bevy)).
- **Bevy 0.20 and Replicon 0.45 from crates.io.** Campfire moves to the Bevy the stack builds on, and its Replicon patch goes, as Replicon 0.45 holds its fix.
- **The stack's benches take Campfire's scheme.** One bench target for each crate, and ids `<kind>/<group>/<case>`, the crate in the group ([One bench target for each crate](02-engine-core.md#benches)); the fork's `Scale`, its one sampling policy, stays the stack's bench helper. Refused: the facade's one bench target for every crate, whose ids carry a fourth part.
- **The stack's documents become Campfire's.** The fork's design documents become the system document of the stack, `13-net-stack/`, each decision kept, its citations in Campfire's form; the decisions of `11-quic-transport.md` that are Campfire's own move into [Sessions](08-sessions.md), and the document goes.

### Planned

- **The harness on the stepper.** Planned: `InProcessMatch` runs its server and its clients on the stack's stepper, which owns time by discrete events, each app at its own frame rate, so a harness match can drift and stall an app as the stack's own tests do. It waits for the slice to end, as it changes how a harness match's frames interleave, and so its goldens.

## Checks

- The stack's 927 tests pass under `cargo drive test`, each within its bound of 1 s, with the workspace's lints and bans.
- `rules`: the layer test places each stack crate, and refuses an engine crate under a problem crate; the crates table names each; each crate `clippy.toml` is the workspace's plus its own entries.
- A tick span, a tick rate and an overstep convert as the fork's tests of them pin, the step of 2⁻²⁴ tick shown; half of 21 Hz runs 21 ticks in 2 s exactly; the sim's conversions of milliseconds to ticks are unchanged.
- A message and a component encode as `codec` encodes them, and a non-canonical one is refused on receipt.
- The harness's matches, the 3v3 scripted match among them, keep their goldens; a golden that moves names its batch and its cause.
- The LAN check passes over the stack's UDP on Linux, macOS and Windows, and each platform's verifier verifies each platform's match.
- A singleplayer match pauses and runs at each speed, its ticks and the client's prediction in step.
