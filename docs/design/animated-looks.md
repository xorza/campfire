# Animated looks

A slice's proposal. At its end, a player of a Zero Hour skirmish sees infantry stand, idle, walk, aim and fire with the original's animations, and die with them; a building rise out of the ground as its dozer builds it and show its damage; and an upgrade change what its units show, as a power plant's rods and a weapon set's model. Its plan is `PLAN.md`'s Animated looks; when the slice ends, each lasting decision moves into the document of the system it governs, and this file is deleted. Its facts are [Zero Hour's animation](../research/zero-hour.md#animation).

## Decisions

- **An animation is a glTF file of its own.** The import converts each W3D animation a look names into a `.glb` of its hierarchy's nodes and its tracks, a key a frame at 30 frames a second, linear, and a time-coded channel's keys at their frames, step where W3D's binary move says so; each model of that hierarchy plays it, as W3D keeps an animation apart from its models and names it by its hierarchy. Refused: the animations copied into each model's file, which repeats an infantry skeleton's dozens of animations in each of its models.
- **A skin is a glTF skin.** Each skinned vertex binds to its one bone at a weight of 1, of that bone's inverse bind matrix, so a moving bone moves it, where the import now places it at its bind pose for good. W3D's skins bind each vertex to one bone, so no weight is lost.
- **A visibility channel hides its pivot's own meshes.** glTF animates no visibility, so the animation file keeps each pivot's visibility keys in its extras, and the client hides the meshes of that pivot alone while a key hides them, as W3D hides a pivot's sub-objects. Refused: a scale of 0 on the pivot, which hides its child pivots' meshes too.
- **A look is states of conditions.** A look lists states, each a set of condition names with its models, the nodes it shows and hides, and its animation; a unit draws the state whose conditions best match the unit's, by the rule night and snow already use (`SparseMatchFinder::findBestInfoSlow`), as Zero Hour's draw modules pick a condition state and OpenRA's `WithInfantryBody` a sequence. A map's conditions become conditions each of its units holds, so night and snow are states too.
- **A condition is the game's name for a test.** A look file names each condition and its test of what every client holds of a unit: a tag; a map condition; moving; an attack's pre-attack, strike, wait between shots or reload, of a weapon slot; its life below a share; a site that waits for its builder, is being built, or is unfinished; and dying. The engine names no game's flag, and the import maps Zero Hour's onto these tests, `DAMAGED` to life below the 0.7 of `UnitDamagedThreshold`. Refused: Zero Hour's flags as the engine's own names, which a capability may not hold ([Games](04-capabilities/games.md)).
- **A state plays its animation by its mode.** A loop, once, either of them backwards, or held at its first or last frame; from its first frame, its last, or one drawn at random; at a speed each unit draws once from its state's range; an idle animation drawn as each loop ends; and between two states of transition keys, their transition's animation first, as Zero Hour's `W3DModelDraw` plays them. Bevy's `AnimationPlayer` plays each, by an `AnimationGraph` for each look and `AnimationTransitions`, Bevy's own mechanisms, which the client gains by Bevy's `bevy_animation` feature. Refused: a sampler of the client's own, which repeats Bevy's.
- **A building rises with its site.** A state may sink its models by the share of its site's time not yet built, of the height of its type's body, as Zero Hour's `ADJUST_HEIGHT_BY_CONSTRUCTION_PERCENT`; so a site's progress reaches every client that holds its unit, where its owner alone receives it now. Refused: the share derived from the site's life, which a hit breaks; the owner's sites alone, where an enemy's build would look finished from its first tick.
- **An upgrade's look is a tag.** A state tests the tags an upgrade gives: `WEAPONSET_PLAYER_UPGRADE` is `weapon_upgrade`'s, and `PowerPlantUpgrade`, `ModelConditionUpgrade` and `SubObjectsUpgrade` convert to modifiers of tags their unit's states test, a sub-object upgrade's tag showing and hiding its nodes; what else they do, as a power bonus, stays a loss ([Zero Hour](09-zero-hour.md), Upgrades' other effects).
- **A death keeps its unit for its animation.** A type's death may keep its dead unit for a time before it despawns, a body of no collision, no target and no order, so every client draws its dying state, as Zero Hour's `SlowDeathBehavior` keeps its object for its `DestructionDelay`. Refused: a client's own copy of a unit that despawns, which no client can tell from a unit that leaves its sight.
- **Doors stay closed.** A production exit's doors need the tick each trained unit leaves at every client, which no state holds; they stay a loss.

## Checks

- The importer's tests convert a hand-written animation, of position, rotation, a time-coded channel and a visibility channel, keys computed by hand; a skin, its vertices bound by their bones; and a draw module's condition states, of each mode and flag, into a look's states and conditions.
- The client's tests pick the state of each set of conditions, compute each condition from a unit's parts, and give each mode's time at a given tick.
- The Zero Hour check counts the animations, the skins and the states converted and dropped, by cause; its skirmish play pins a site's rise and a Ranger's states as it walks, fires and dies.
- In the client, a player sees a Ranger idle, walk, fire and die, a barracks rise as its dozer builds it and show its damage, and a power plant's rods after Advanced Control Rods.
