# Animated looks

A slice's proposal. At its end, a player of a Zero Hour skirmish sees infantry stand, idle, walk, aim and fire with the original's animations, and die with them; a building rise out of the ground as its dozer builds it and show its damage; and an upgrade change what its units show, as a power plant's rods and a weapon set's model. Its plan is `PLAN.md`'s Animated looks; when the slice ends, each lasting decision moves into the document of the system it governs, and this file is deleted. Its facts are [Zero Hour's animation](../research/zero-hour.md#animation).

## Decisions

- **An animation is a glTF file of its own.** The import converts each W3D animation a look names into a `.glb` of its hierarchy's nodes and its tracks, a key a frame at 30 frames a second, linear, and a time-coded channel's keys at their frames, so no key is sampled again, its last key at its last frame, as W3D's frame never passes it ([research](../research/zero-hour.md#animation)); each model of that hierarchy plays it, as W3D keeps an animation apart from its models and names it by its hierarchy. A key that steps, a time-coded visibility channel and an Euler turn are refused, as no file of the install holds one ([research](../research/zero-hour.md#animation)). Refused: the animations copied into each model's file, which repeats an infantry skeleton's dozens of animations in each of its models.
- **A skin is a glTF skin.** Each skinned vertex binds to its one bone at a weight of 1, of that bone's inverse bind matrix, so a moving bone moves it, where the import now places it at its bind pose for good. W3D's skins bind each vertex to one bone, so no weight is lost.
- **A visibility channel hides its pivot's own meshes.** glTF animates no visibility, so the animation file keeps the frames each pivot is hidden in in the vendor extension `CAMPFIRE_animation_extras` on its animation, as a material's extras ([Material extras in a glTF extension](02-engine-core.md#client)), and the client hides the meshes of that pivot alone while a frame hides them, as W3D hides a pivot's sub-objects. Refused: a scale of 0 on the pivot, which hides its child pivots' meshes too.
- **A look is states of conditions.** A look lists states, each a set of condition names with its models, the nodes it shows and hides, and its animation; a unit draws the state whose conditions best match the unit's, by the rule night and snow already use (`SparseMatchFinder::findBestInfoSlow`), as Zero Hour's draw modules pick a condition state and OpenRA's `WithInfantryBody` a sequence. A map's conditions become conditions each of its units holds, so night and snow are states too.
- **A look's draws are its draw modules.** A look holds a draw for each of its object's draw modules, each of its own states and the conditions it ignores (`IgnoreConditionStates`), and a unit draws each one's best state, as a Zero Hour drawable draws each module apart. Refused: one list of states for the whole look, whose states would be the product of its modules'.
- **A condition of no test is never held.** A units file gives each condition a state names its test, `never` for one no test of the engine's says, as `RUBBLE` or `GARRISONED`, so a state of it counts it as lacked, as the original counts a flag its object does not hold, and a file that names a condition of no entry fails to read. Refused: dropping the state, which the original may still draw when its other conditions match best.
- **A state's new models replace the old as they are ready.** A unit's state of other models spawns them hidden, and they replace the models before as their scenes are dressed; a model of the same scene stays, and takes its new hidden nodes. So a state's scenes load as a unit first takes it, and each look's rest models alone as the match starts, as most of a look's models are of states few units take ([research](../research/zero-hour.md#animation)). Refused: despawning the old models at once, which draws the unit of nothing for the frames its new scenes take to spawn; and every state's models loaded as the match starts.
- **A condition is the game's name for a test.** A look file names each condition and its test of what every client holds of a unit: a tag; a map condition; moving; its life below a share; a site before or after a builder first built it, and the engine's tag `being_built` of a site that grew in the tick; an attack's pre-attack, shot, wait between shots or reload, of a group of its look's weapons; and dying. The engine names no game's flag, and the import maps Zero Hour's onto these tests: `DAMAGED` to a life above the 0.35 of `UnitReallyDamagedThreshold` and at most the 0.7 of `UnitDamagedThreshold`, as `calcDamageState` sets one damage state; `MOVING` to a walk that moved within a quarter of a second, as the original clears it from an object held up for longer; `AWAITING_CONSTRUCTION`, `PARTIALLY_CONSTRUCTED` and `ACTIVELY_BEING_CONSTRUCTED` to a site's stage and its tag, as `DozerAIUpdate` sets them; and a weapon slot's `FIRING_A` and its kin to the look's group of the weapons of slot A in each of its sets ([research](../research/zero-hour.md#animation)). Refused: Zero Hour's flags as the engine's own names, which a capability may not hold ([Games](04-capabilities/games.md)).
- **The client derives an attack's phase.** A client derives each phase from what it holds, as `Object::updateDrawableWeaponState` sets it: the action's progress gives the attack, the pre-attack and the shot under way; a slot's ready tick and its clip's shots give the wait and the reload; and a rise of the ready tick it sees gives the tick of a shot. Zero Hour's rules show every player the slots' times and clips, as the original draws every unit's reload; a mode that hides them draws an enemy's reload as its wait. Refused: a state part of the attack's phase for every client, which the sim would write at each phase's end for its look alone.
- **A state plays its animation by its mode.** A loop, once, a ping-pong, either of the first two backwards, or held; from its mode's frame, its first, its last, one drawn at random, or the frame of the state before in a group it keeps its frame across; at a speed each unit draws once from its state's range, or at its unit's walk by its stride; idle clips drawn as each completes; a state of a key that waits for the clip before to complete; and between two states of transition keys, their transition's clip first, as Zero Hour's `W3DModelDraw` plays them ([research](../research/zero-hour.md#animation)). The client counts each unit's frame by W3D's rules (`Compute_Current_Frame`), and Bevy's `AnimationPlayer`, of Bevy's `bevy_animation` feature, poses its model at that frame by a graph of the clip alone. Refused: Bevy's own clock, whose repeat wraps at a clip's end, not its last frame, and has no ping-pong, no completion of a backward clip and no frame kept across states; a graph of each look's clips, which blends, where W3D cuts from one clip to the next.
- **A building rises with its site.** A state may name a rise, and sinks its models by it times the share of its site's time not yet built, as Zero Hour's `ADJUST_HEIGHT_BY_CONSTRUCTION_PERCENT` sinks its scaled model by its geometry's height, which the import names; so a site and its progress are of the `unit` kind, which every client that holds the unit receives, where its owner alone received them. Refused: the height of the type's body, which a small structure lacks; the share derived from the site's life, which a hit breaks; the owner's sites alone, where an enemy's build would look finished from its first tick.
- **An upgrade's look is a tag.** A state tests the tags an upgrade gives: `WEAPONSET_PLAYER_UPGRADE` is `weapon_upgrade`'s; `PowerPlantUpgrade` and `ModelConditionUpgrade` give the tag of the condition they set, of its name, which the states of that condition test; and `SubObjectsUpgrade` gives a tag of its first trigger's, by which each of its object's models shows and hides its nodes over its state's, in order, as `W3DModelDraw::updateSubObjects` does. What else they do, as a power bonus and the rods' extension, which `PowerPlantUpdate` draws under `POWER_PLANT_UPGRADING` for its `RodsExtendTime`, stays a loss ([Zero Hour](09-zero-hour.md), Upgrades' other effects). Refused: a state of each combination of upgrades, which multiplies the states.
- **A death keeps its unit for its animation.** A type's `on_death` may keep its dead unit for a time, `{ keep_ms = <ms> }`, a body of no collision, no target and no order, as every pass already passes over a dead unit that stays; the server despawns it at the end of the tick its time ends, by a timer of its own the client never receives, so every client draws its dying state, as Zero Hour's `SlowDeathBehavior` keeps its object for its `DestructionDelay`. The import keeps each object for the delay of the slow death a normal death most likely takes, of the first veterancy and no status, the one of most weight, the first of a tie; the draw among several by weight and overkill, the delay's variance, a death of another type or of an object under a status, as a site's, which a module's `ExemptStatus` may destroy at once, and the kin of `SlowDeathBehavior`, as a helicopter's crash, stay a loss ([research](../research/zero-hour.md#animation)). Refused: a client's own copy of a unit that despawns, which no client can tell from a unit that leaves its sight; a timer of every client, which the server's despawn already reaches.
- **Doors stay closed.** A production exit's doors need the tick each trained unit leaves at every client, which no state holds; they stay a loss.

## Checks

- The importer's tests convert a hand-written animation, of position, rotation, a time-coded channel and a visibility channel, keys computed by hand; a skin, its vertices bound by their bones; and a draw module's condition states, of each mode and flag, into a look's states and conditions.
- The client's tests pick the state of each set of conditions, compute each condition from a unit's parts, and give each mode's time at a given tick.
- The Zero Hour check counts the animations, the skins and the states converted and dropped, by cause; its skirmish play pins a site's rise and a Ranger's states as it walks, fires and dies.
- In the client, a player sees a Ranger idle, walk, fire and die, a barracks rise as its dozer builds it and show its damage, and a power plant's rods after Advanced Control Rods.
