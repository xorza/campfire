# Roads

A slice's proposal. At its end, a player sees a Zero Hour map's roads, sidewalks, tracks and markings in the client, laid over the terrain as the game draws them, so the plaza of Tournament Desert shows its sidewalk and gravel road over the terrain's hard edges. Its plan is `PLAN.md`'s Roads; when the slice ends, each lasting decision moves into the document of the system it governs, and this file is deleted. Its facts are [Zero Hour's roads](../research/zero-hour-roads.md).

## Decisions

- **A road is a decal the import meshes.** The import builds each map's roads as the game's road buffer does, its chains, curves, junctions and joins between types, and writes their triangles, each vertex at its ground position, its height and its place in the texture; the client draws them as given. Refused: the road rules in the client, a rule of one game in the pipeline every game shares ([One rendering pipeline for every game](../../AGENTS.md#direction)); each road as a model among the map's client objects, which would cast shadows, take the pointer, and blend in no fixed order with the terrain's overlays.
- **Decals are the terrain file's, after its overlays.** The terrain file holds the decals as one mesh in drawing order, each vertex's position and its texture coordinates in the terrain's texture, and the client appends them to the overlay mesh, so one draw blends the terrain's blends, then the decals, in the file's order, as the game draws its roads after its terrain in stacking order. Refused: a mesh for each road type, which Bevy's transparent phase orders by its bounds' centre and depth bias, so an order that moves with the camera; an order of the client's own, which no standard material takes.
- **A decal's texture joins the terrain's.** Each road texture a map uses is a copy in the terrain's texture, beside the game's 2,048 texels of terrain classes, the texture growing to 4,096 texels wide, wrapped by a border on every side; the import splits each decal triangle where its coordinates cross a whole repeat of its texture, so a road that repeats along its length samples its own copy, as the game's repeated texture does. Refused: a texture array and a material of our own, past Bevy's standard material; coordinates clamped to one repeat, which stretches a long road's texture.
- **A decal floats as the game's.** The import lays each piece as the game does: a column for each map cell along it, both its vertices at the highest cell corner across it plus an eighth of a height step, a column dropped where the line between its neighbours stays above the ground within 1.1 steps. Refused: the client's height at each vertex, which a straight edge between two vertices cuts under on a slope, as the game's highest corner does not.
- **A decal is lit as the ground under it.** The client gives each decal vertex the normal of the height grid's sample nearest it, as the game lights a road's vertex by the terrain's light at its nearest sample, and the terrain's surface. Refused: a normal stored in the file, which the grid gives already.
- **Every road draws.** The client draws every decal of the map, where the game drops a road type's visible pieces past 3,000 vertices or 5,000 indices in a frame. Refused: the game's limits, which drop roads by where the camera looks.
- **A road's type is `Roads.ini`'s.** A segment's template names its type, whose `Texture`, `RoadWidth` and `RoadWidthInTexture` size and texture it; a segment of a template no type names, 3 in the install, draws nothing and is a loss of the import, as the game draws it with its first type only in a build of test assets. Bridges stay as the import places them now.

## Checks

- The import's tests mesh a straight segment, a curve, a tight curve and a miter, a tee, a Y, an H and a four-way, and an alpha join between two types, to hand-computed vertices, heights and texture coordinates, and split a long segment's triangles at its texture's repeats.
- The Zero Hour check pins Tournament Desert's road pieces by kind and its decal triangles, and its import report lists the segments of no type.
- The client's tests append a terrain file's decals to the overlay mesh after its overlays, at their positions, with the normals of their nearest samples.
- In the client, a player sees Tournament Desert's sidewalk and gravel road around its central plaza, and its roads elsewhere, as the game draws them.
