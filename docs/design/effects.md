# Effects

A slice's proposal. At its end, a player of a Zero Hour skirmish sees each shot's fire effect at its barrel, each projectile's exhaust as it flies, a hit's burst where a projectile detonates or a weapon of no projectile strikes, and each death's explosion, as the game's FX lists and particle systems draw them. Its plan is `PLAN.md`'s Effects; when the slice ends, its lasting decisions fold into `02-engine-core.md` and `09-zero-hour.md`, and this document goes. The facts are [Zero Hour's effects](../research/zero-hour-effects.md).

## Decisions

- **An effect is the client's, from the state it holds.** The sim sends nothing for an effect: the client plays a shot's effect at the tick it derives the shot from its slots, as it derives an attack's phase, a hit's where a projectile it held ends or a shot of no projectile reaches its target, and a death's as a unit gains `Dead` (02-engine-core.md, The client derives an attack's phase). Refused: an event of the sim for each effect, which costs every link bytes for what the client already holds, as the game's FX are its client's alone (`FXList.cpp`).
- **Effects are a format of the engine.** The client files gain particle systems and effect lists, each by name: a system's emission, the life, size, alpha and color of its particles over that life, its forces and its blend; a list's systems, each with its count, offset, radius, height and delay; and beside the looks, an action's fire effect and its hit of a shot of no projectile, a projectile type's end and trail, as the client knows a projectile's type and not the action that fired it, and a unit type's death effect. A game's import converts its own, and the format names no game. Refused: the game's INI read in the client, a format of one game on the client's path.
- **Particles run on the CPU and draw as billboards of the one material.** Each frame the client steps its particles, then writes one mesh of camera-facing quads, or ground-aligned ones, for each texture and blend, drawn through the standard material in the blends the client files already name, `Alpha`, `Add` and `Multiply`, and an alpha test as a mask. Refused: a GPU particle crate, a new dependency whose expressions do not hold the game's keys and damping exactly, where a few thousand particles cost little on the CPU, as the original and OpenSAGE step theirs there; a shader of one game, against AGENTS.md's one rendering pipeline for every game.
- **A system steps as its file says.** The effects file names the length of a step, and a system steps in whole steps of it on the client's clock, as Zero Hour's import writes 1/30 s, the game's logic frame, so a rate, a damping and a key's time mean what the data says at every frame rate: emission in bursts after an initial delay through the system's life, a particle's start in its volume, hollow or whole, and its velocity by its kind, then each step its velocity damped and pulled by gravity and drift, its size grown by its rate, damped, and its alpha and color between their keys (`ParticleSys.cpp`). Refused: steps of the frame's length, under which damping per step changes with the frame rate.
- **A cap by priority, from the file.** The effects file names a cap of particles and gives each system a rank of priority, or none for a system that passes the cap: a new particle past the cap removes the oldest of a lower rank first, as the game's `ParticleSystemManager::removeOldestParticles` does. Zero Hour's import writes the install's `MaxParticleCount`, 2,500, its priorities' order as ranks, and `ALWAYS_RENDER` as none. Refused: a cap or a list of priorities in the client, constants of one game in the engine; a cap of the client's own, which would keep a different effect when a battle crowds them.
- **A shot's effect at its barrel.** A look's state names, for each weapon slot, its barrels, one for each number 01, 02 and on that any of the slot's bones holds, or one of the bare names, each with the bone of its `WeaponFireFXBone` its fire effect plays at, and the client plays each shot at the next barrel of the slot, of the state it draws at the shot, back to the first after the last, or at the unit's position for a barrel of no bone or a slot of no barrel, as the game's weapon plays an effect its draw does not (`W3DModelDraw::handleWeaponFireFX`, `validateWeaponBarrelInfo`, `Weapon.cpp`), as Total Annihilation, Spring and Supreme Commander keep one fire point for each barrel and the index of the next (decided 2026-10-26). Refused: the unit's position always, which shows every tank's flash at its center; the first barrel always, which flashes a twin barrel from one side.
- **The import hands the client consistent effects.** The import trims what nothing plays, as a system no list or system names; drops a nugget or a slave that names no system, and a system whose texture no archive holds, as a counted loss; orders a system's keys by time, and stores a texture two systems share once; so the effects file holds no dangling name, and its load refuses one rather than skip it (AGENTS.md's Direction, the importer takes a game's data as it is). Refused: a client that skips what does not resolve, a workaround for one game's data in the engine.
- **What the slice leaves.** Systems of `STREAK`, `VOLUME_PARTICLE` and `DRAWABLE` particles, 35 of 1,084, and wind motion, 8; nuggets of sound, view shake, light pulse, terrain scorch, tracer, ray effect and FX at a bone, 596 of 1,466; veterancy's effects; a die module's choice by death type, which plays the first effect of no condition; and the systems a model's states name on their bones, as a damaged building's smoke: each a loss of the import, counted.

## Checks

- The import's tests convert a particle system of each emission volume and velocity, and an FX list of each nugget kind, to the engine's format or a counted loss, trim a system nothing plays and drop a nugget of no system; the effects file's load refuses a name that resolves to nothing; the Zero Hour check pins the converted systems, lists, textures and losses.
- The client's tests step a system to hand-computed particles at the file's step, at two lengths of it: a burst's count and times, a particle's start in a hollow cylinder, its velocity damped and pulled, its size, alpha and color between keys, and a cap that removes the oldest of the lowest priority.
- The client's tests play a weapon's fire effect at its state's bone, its hit effect where a projectile ends, its trail on the projectile, and a death effect as a unit gains `Dead`.
- In the client, a player of Tournament Desert sees a tank's shot flash at its barrel, a missile's exhaust behind it, its hit's burst, and a destroyed building's explosion.
