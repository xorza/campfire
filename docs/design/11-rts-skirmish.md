# Campfire — RTS skirmish

What the first half of Stage 7 adds so that a tiny RTS skirmish plays to its golden: box bodies, unit types in every package, group orders, and production's first cut, its supply, requirements, cancel, rally, construction and gathering. The rules live in the capability documents this one points to; this one gives the decisions, the skirmish mode and the match that prove them, their cost, and what waits. The RTS mission, with region events, objectives, carry and save converters, is the second half of the stage, with a proposal of its own.

## Decisions

- **D1. A building's body is a box, in every system.** A unit type that does not walk, on a planar map, may have a box body in place of a circle: pathing, placement, collision and reach all read the box, as 0 A.D. keeps a structure as a static obstruction square while a unit stays a circle ([Space and map](04-capabilities/00-overview.md#space-and-map)). The other way, StarCraft II's, keeps a radius for reach and a footprint for pathing beside it; it costs less, and a range to a building's long side is then wrong by up to the difference between the two shapes. A box needs no second shape for any test, so every capability that measures reach measures the same building.
- **D2. A box turns to any angle, and its shape rounds once, as it spawns.** A box's angle is in degrees: a multiple of 90° turns its half edges exactly, and any other angle turns them by math's sine and cosine; each component of a half edge is then rounded once to a `Num`. The rounded half edges are the box: every test after reads them exactly, in wide integers, so the rounding is the shape's, and no test rounds again. 0 A.D. keeps a structure as its centre and two half-edge vectors in fixed point. Degrees, not radians, make the right angles a builder uses most exact, as no multiple of π/2 is a `Num`.
- **D3. Construction has three styles, chosen by the build action.** `alone`: the building grows by itself once placed, as StarCraft II's Protoss buildings do. `builder`: one builder stays for the whole time, and the building waits while it has none, as a Terran SCV builds. `builders = [...]`: many builders together, at the rate the table gives for their count, the last entry for any count past it, as Age of Empires and 0 A.D. speed a site with each builder, less for each one more. A table is exact, and it says any curve a mode wants: 0 A.D.'s n^0.7 has no exact form in fixed point, so a table states it as the mode rounds it, `["1", "1.62", "2.16", "2.64"]` up to four builders.
- **D4. One gatherer at a node; gatherers pass through each other.** A node takes one gatherer at a time. A worker that finds its node taken goes to the nearest free node of the same resource near it, or waits, as a StarCraft II worker moves to another mineral patch. Workers in the gather loop do not collide with each other, so the income does not depend on the order collision parts them in.
- **D5. Supply is derived, not a player resource.** A unit type costs supply and may provide it; a player's supply used is what its living units and its queued trains cost, and its cap what its complete providers give, within the mode's maximum. No script keeps it: a death, a cancel or a completion changes it through the units themselves, so it cannot drift from them. A train reserves its supply when it joins the queue, as it pays its whole cost then ([Production](04-capabilities/production.md#supply)).
- **D6. Every package may hold unit types that stand, each in its own scope.** An avatar's summon, a loadout's ward and a faction's units are unit types of their own packages, which only their packages' data and scripts name. The skirmish's factions stay in the mode's package; a faction as a package of its own, which a mode offers as a choice, waits ([Unit types](08-script-api.md#data-files)).
- **D7. One command orders a group, which shares one route.** An order names its units, so a group's move is one command, and the group plans one long route. Each unit keeps its place in the group when the goal lies outside the box of their positions, and goes to the goal itself when it lies inside, as StarCraft II's selection keeps its formation only for a move away from it ([Groups](04-capabilities/navigation.md#groups)). A wave of a spawn group shares its route the same way.
- **D8. A seen enemy body blocks a placement; a friendly or hidden one is moved out.** A placement fails where its box overlaps a wall, a static body, or the body of a walker whose team is not friendly to the builder's and that the builder's group sees. A refusal for a hidden walker would tell the builder it is there, so a hidden enemy is moved out as a friendly walker is, as the cost of telling nothing: a player may move a hidden enemy by building on it. A walker in the box goes, as the building spawns, to the nearest cell it may stand in, as a teleport places a unit, with no disjoint. Collision would push it out too, but a walker between the new box and another static body, closer than its width, would be pushed from one into the other on every tick; the grid's nearest cell is clear of all of them, so it lands once and stays.

- **D9. Each player's town hall and workers spawn from the mode's script; a placed node proves a placed angle.** A map's placed unit has a team and no player, and a town hall needs one: it trains, pays and takes loads for its player. So `on_match_start` spawns each player's town hall and workers at its team's start marker, as StarCraft II's melee start creates each player's town hall and workers at a start location, not in the map. Its town hall stands at angle 0, as `ctx.spawn_unit` gives. A mineral node, a placed unit of the neutral team, stands at 30°, so a placed angle still reaches every system that reads a box. An owner on placed units, or an angle in `ctx.spawn_unit`, would add data or a script form that no rule here needs.
- **D10. The verifier's own test writes the skirmish's log for the other platforms.** The verifier's headless test plays the skirmish to its end, as it plays the lane and the 3v3, and replays the log's file with the packages a verifier holds, in process and through the verifier's binary, on every run. With `CAMPFIRE_MATCH_LOGS` set to a directory, it also keeps the log there as `skirmish.log`, and its checkpoint's snapshot beside it. CI sets it in each platform's test step and keeps the directory, and each platform's verifier replays every platform's log, with its snapshots, as it replays the LAN check's. So a log that does not verify fails on the machine that wrote it, before CI, and CI adds only the platforms. The golden proves that each platform plays the same hashes; the replay proves that the log and the snapshot one platform writes decode and verify on another, which no golden touches, as 0 A.D. checks a desync fix by replaying a recorded match with `-replay` on another build.
- **D11. The skirmish is an end case.** `server_tick/first_skirmish`, `worst_skirmish` and `mean_skirmish`, and `server_stage/worst_skirmish_<stage>` and `mean_skirmish_<stage>`, play the scripted match as the 3v3's cases play theirs ([Benches](02-engine-core.md#benches)). A kernel case measures one path on a made scene; the end case measures what the rules cost together in a match. Its numbers go in [Cost](#cost), production's Act and Mode stages in [Production](04-capabilities/production.md#cost), and navigation's Move and Collide stages in [Navigation](04-capabilities/navigation.md#cost).
- **D12. The match asserts one timeline.** An observer reads each tick's state and events into typed events, each with its tick: a load delivered and its amount, a node held, waited at, run out and despawned, a train joined, refused, cancelled and spawned, a site placed, paused, resumed and complete, a placement refused, a walker moved out of a box, a building first seen by a team, a group's goals, a crossing of the wall, a death, the match's end. The test asserts that the whole timeline equals the expected one, which it builds from hand-computed ticks: an event the match adds, loses or moves fails with the first difference, and no event goes unchecked because no assertion looked for it. Assertions of single ticks, as the lane's are, check only what they name.
- **D13. The script follows the events, and every timed walk is exact.** A scene's orders go out on a tick, or on the tick after the observer records the event that ends the scene before it, so no order is sent before what it needs, and a change of one scene's length moves the later ones with it rather than breaking them. The orders the script sends are in the log, so the golden and the replay pin them whatever triggered them. Every time is a multiple of a tick, 50 ms; every speed over the tick rate is exact in a `Num`, as 5 m/s gives 0.25 m a tick, so no step rounds; and every walk whose ticks an event depends on is a straight line on open ground, so its ticks are its distance over its step, rounded up. A walk round the wall or through a crowd is asserted only by where it ends.
- **D14. The restore and order tests sweep the skirmish too.** The archetype-shuffle test, the snapshot tests, byte flips and drawn values, and the resume from each checkpoint run over the proving match and the skirmish, as one sweep, not as new tests. The skirmish checkpoints four times, each where a part of production's state stands: in scene 2 a held node, a waiting worker and a load; in scene 4 a paused `builder` site; in scene 6 a `builders` site; in scene 8 a queue of five with its paid amounts, an `alone` site and two rally points. The resume runs from each, and the snapshot tests flip and draw the bytes of scene 8's, so a restore meets every state type production adds, which the proving match does not reach.

## The skirmish mode

A test mode, `source/packages/test/modes/skirmish`, owned by the engine's tests: two players of one faction on a small map with a wall between them, a neutral team that holds the mineral nodes, and ground and air layers. It is not a game. Each capability it declares is one the [StarCraft row](04-capabilities/genres.md#target-games) needs: `combat`, `stats`, `projectiles`, `orders`, `navigation`, `vision` and `production`. It has no AI script: every unit acts on its player's orders only, so each event comes from the script.

| Unit type | Body | What it proves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Town hall | Box, 5 × 5 m | A drop-off; trains workers; provides supply; spawned for its player by the mode's script |
| Worker | Circle | Gathers; builds in each of the three styles; passes through other gatherers |
| Mineral node | Box, 2 × 1 m | A node of a neutral team that depletes and despawns; one is a box turned at a placed angle, 30° |
| Depot | Box, 2 × 2 m | Provides supply only once complete; built by one `builder`, at 45° |
| Barracks | Box, 3 × 3 m | Requires a depot; built by `builders`; trains soldiers; a rally point |
| Airfield | Box, 4 × 3 m | Requires a barracks; grows `alone`; trains a flyer |
| Soldier | Circle, ground | A weapon that reaches a box from its edge |
| Flyer | Circle, air | Flies over the wall and over ground units, which block no air body |

**The map.** Planar, 64 × 40 m, with a vision grid of 1 m cells and a pathing grid of 0.5 m cells. A ground wall runs north to south through the middle, with a gap at its south end, so a ground unit walks round it and an air unit flies over it. Each team has a start marker, west and east. The west's three nodes stand in a row north of its start, one at 30°, with open ground straight between them and its town hall; the east's one node stands alone, with no other node within its gather's `bounce`. A brush stands south of the east's start, so a unit in it is hidden from the units outside it ([Vision](04-capabilities/vision.md)). Each scene has its own part of the map, so two scenes that run at once meet no unit of each other's.

**The mode's script.** `on_match_start` gives each player its town hall, four workers and its first minerals. A unit with the `building` tag that dies ends the match when its player owns no other living one, sites included, found by `ctx.find` from the unit that died: its enemy team wins. The town hall provides four supply, as many as its workers use; a depot provides eight.

## The skirmish match

One run, as [one scripted match for each mode](02-engine-core.md#testing-and-diagnostics) asks, at the scripted matches' 20 ticks a second. The script names units by role, as the proving match does: a player's town hall, its workers by stable id, its sites and buildings by type, the nodes by place. West, player 0, builds and wins; east, player 1, plays the refusals and loses. Each scene's events join the timeline (D12):

1. **Income.** West's workers gather at its three nodes, the third sent to a node another holds: it goes on to the nearest free one. Each load joins west's minerals at the town hall in the tick the trip's time gives, exact by the trip.
2. **A node that runs out.** Two east workers gather at its lone node: the second waits, as no free node is near, and holds the node in the tick the first's gather ends. The node holds two loads: it despawns in the Vision stage of the tick its last load is taken, and the worker whose node is gone delivers its load and stops.
3. **Supply and a requirement.** West's town hall orders a worker with four of four supply used: the train is refused, and nothing is paid. A worker orders a barracks before any depot: the build is refused for its requirement, and nothing is paid.
4. **A builder.** A west worker builds a depot at 45° on a point where another west worker stands: the site spawns, its life at `start_life`, and the friendly walker lands on the nearest open cell in that tick. The builder is sent away: the site's progress stops from the next tick. Another worker comes to the site and the progress goes on.
5. **The depot completes.** West's cap rises by eight. West's town hall rallies to a node; the worker train and the barracks build, ordered again, are accepted; the worker spawns on the rally point's side of the town hall, walks to the node and gathers by its first gather.
6. **Builders.** Two west workers build the barracks: its progress grows by the table's entry for one, then for two from the tick the second arrives.
7. **Alone.** A west worker places the airfield, which needs the barracks, and is free at once; the site grows by itself.
8. **A cancel at each place.** West's barracks rallies to a point near the wall's gap and queues five soldiers. A cancel of the middle entry refunds what it paid; a cancel past the queue's end is ignored; a cancel of the head refunds it, and the next entry starts in the same tick. The three left spawn and walk to the rally point. The last checkpoint of D14 falls in this scene.
9. **A seen enemy, a hidden one.** A west worker walks to the east's start. An east worker orders a depot on it in plain sight: the build is refused, and nothing is paid. The west worker steps into the brush; the same build, ordered again, places its site, and the hidden west worker lands on the nearest open cell.
10. **An edge in sight.** An east worker walks to where its sight reveals only the cells along one edge of west's town hall, a box at angle 0, whose cells a hand computes: the town hall is first seen by the east in that tick, with its centre beyond the worker's sight.
11. **Groups.** West's three soldiers move as a group to a point outside the box of their positions: each goal is that point plus its offset, computed by hand. Then to a point inside their box: every goal is that point.
12. **The flyer.** The airfield trains the flyer, which flies east straight over the wall, on a route of one waypoint, while the soldiers walk round it through the gap.
13. **The end.** East cancels its depot's site: `cancel_refund` of its cost comes back, rounded down, and the site despawns with no death. The soldiers and the flyer attack east's town hall, its last building: it dies, and the mode ends the match, west the winner, in that tick. The session ends, and its result signs the final hash.

The golden plays on every OS in CI, and each platform's verifier replays every platform's log (D10).

## What waits

- **The RTS mission:** region events, `quests` objectives, carry and campaigns, save converters (Stage 7's second half).
- **Order queues** (shift-click) and the `hold` order; formations, flow fields and ORCA crowds (Stage 17).
- **A faction as its own package**, and the `choices` offer that lists them (D6).
- **Gas:** a building placed on a node, as a refinery is on a geyser; creep and power fields beyond the `near` and `away` rules.
- **What each client receives of production**: no client receives queues, supply, loads, a site's progress, a node's amount or player resources until Stage 8 sends them ([Production](04-capabilities/production.md#network)). A box body replicates with this stage, so the client draws it.
- **A client to play it:** the build placement, the gather and rally clicks and the queue's buttons, with the reference game's client (Stage 10). The client draws box bodies from this stage on, so a box is never shown as a circle.
