# Zero Hour projectiles

The first Zero Hour units that fire shells. The player builds a war factory, trains a Crusader, a Battlemaster or a Scorpion, and orders it to attack: its turret turns to the target, the gun fires a shell that flies a curve over the ground, and the shell detonates on the target, on a structure in its way, or where its curve ends, and damages every unit within its radius, the player's own among them. It builds on [Combat](04-capabilities/combat.md), [Zero Hour combat](19-zero-hour-combat.md), [Units that drive](17-units-that-drive.md) and [Terrain](16-terrain.md), and takes the issue log's items of combat's living targets and of the damage a slow weapon delays. The facts it takes from are [Zero Hour research](../research/zero-hour.md#combat), its entry **Tank shells**; the practice of other engines it follows is in [Practice](#practice).

## Decisions

- **D1. The sim's ground is the map's height grid.** The height grid, `map/<name>/heights.bin`, becomes data of the sim: it moves from `client-files` to `geometry` as `Ground`, gains each cell's split, the diagonal its two triangles meet on, which the terrain file held for the client alone (design 17, D8), and loads with its map into `MapBook`, so its bytes enter the match's terms as the map's other data does. The ground's height at a point of the ground plane is the height of the cell's triangle that holds it, the plane through its three samples, computed from the samples in one step and rounded once (design 09, D8); a point off the grid takes the height of the grid's nearest point, as the client's does. A map with no height grid has the ground at height 0. The client reads the same `Ground`, so a unit stands where the sim measures it. On a `planar` map a unit's height in space is its ground's height at its point plus its height above the ground, the position's `y`. `Ground` also holds, on cells of `high_cell` meters from the grid's origin, the map's `[ground] high_cell`, each cell's highest sample among those its square holds, its edges included, as Zero Hour's partition cells of 40 hold the highest of the samples `calcHeights` steps over; `Ground::highest_along(a, b)` is the highest of the cells a line of the ground plane crosses, from `a`'s cell to `b`'s by Bresenham's walk, as `PartitionManager::iterateCellsAlongLine` walks them.
- **D2. A body has a height.** A unit type's `height`, in meters, at least 0 and 0 by default, makes its body a volume: a circle's body a cylinder, a box's a box, from its ground point up by `height`. Its **center** is its ground point raised by half its height, as Zero Hour's `getCenterPosition` raises an object's position by its geometry's half height. A shot aims at its target's center, and a splash measures to the volume (D5).
- **D3. A projectile may fly a curve.** A projectile type's `curve = { first = [<fraction>, <height>], second = [<fraction>, <height>], follow = "<m/s>", life_ms }` makes it fly a cubic Bézier curve in place of a line: its first point its launch, its last its target's center, or the point it aims at, and its two inner points, on the ground plane, at `first`'s and `second`'s fractions of the line from the first point to the last, and in height at `first`'s and `second`'s heights above the highest of the first point, the last, and the ground along the line, `Ground::highest_along`, as Zero Hour's `calcFlightPath` sets them. Its **points** are ⌈`L` ÷ `s`⌉, at least 1, for `s` its speed a tick and `L` the curve's length as `BezierSegment::getApproximateLength` measures it: half its chord plus half its control polygon, the curve split at its middle while the two differ by more than 1 m, each length exact (design 09). In the tick after its launch it is at its first point, and in each tick after at the next, point `k` of `n` the curve's value at `t = k ÷ (n − 1)`, one point being the first, each coordinate computed from the four control points in one step and rounded once, so no tick's rounding carries into the next, as design 13's D13 and OpenRA's `WPos.LerpQuadratic` compute a flight; in the tick after its last point, or as its `life_ms` passes, it **ends** where it is. Before each step, a curve aimed at a living unit moves its last point toward that unit's center, by at most `follow` a second, when the center lies more than √0.1 m from it, and computes its inner points again with its point count kept, as `DumbProjectileBehavior::update` follows its victim. A curve's flight is state its type and its start define, so a snapshot holds its points' inputs, not its points.
- **D4. A curve meets units by its point.** After the tick's movement, each curve projectile's sphere, of half its type's `width`, at its point, meets the volumes of the living units its `hits` filter selects, and its target's always, but never its source's: the first it meets, by stable id, is its hit, and it ends there, as Zero Hour's partition manager tests a shell's sphere against the geometry of what `ProjectileCollidesWith` names after the frame's updates (`PartitionContactList::processContactList`, `shouldProjectileCollideWith`). It tests no path between two points: a sphere that passes a body between two points meets it not, as a shell passes one in Zero Hour. A line projectile keeps its swept test.
- **D5. An attack may splash.** An `attack` action takes `splash = { radius = "<m>", affects = "<relations>", spares_source }`, with a projectile `delivery` alone: at its projectile's end, by a hit, its last point or its life, it damages, by its `amount` and `damage_kind` as a hit would, each living unit of the relations `affects` names, `allies`, `enemies` or `neutrals`, whose volume lies nearer to the end's point than `radius`, in 3D, and its source too unless `spares_source`, `true` by default; the damage is its source's, as Zero Hour's `dealDamageInternal` counts a projectile's damage as its launcher's, and the units take it in stable id order. Its projectile's hit then deals nothing of its own, so the unit it hits takes the splash once. As OpenRA's weapons carry their effects in warheads, `SpreadDamageWarhead` beside its projectile, and 0 A.D.'s attacks a `Splash`, the splash is data of the weapon, and no script runs for it. The units within the radius come from the spatial index's query near the point.
- **D6. An attack may scatter.** An `attack` action takes `scatter = { radius = "<m>", tags = { <tag> = "<m>", ... } }`, with a projectile `delivery` alone: a shot at a target of a tag the table names adds that tag's radius, the largest of them when it holds several, to `radius`; when the sum is above 0, the shot aims at a point of the ground at a distance drawn from 0 to the sum and an angle drawn from 0 to a turn, in that order, from its target's ground point, on its own stream of the sim's random source, and its projectile follows no unit, as Zero Hour's `fireWeaponTemplate` scatters a weapon at infantry: it may miss, and detonate on the ground or on a unit its `hits` filter selects. The engine's random sequence is not Zero Hour's, so its shots fall elsewhere than the original's with the same seed.
- **D7. An attack may bound its pitch.** An `attack` action takes `pitch_deg = [<low>, <high>]`: a target whose center lies below `low` or above `high` degrees as seen from its attacker's center, against the ground plane, is out of its reach, as `MinTargetPitch` and `MaxTargetPitch` bound a weapon's targets.
- **D8. A unit type may have a turret.** A unit type's `turret = { turn_rate, tolerance_deg, slot }` puts its attacks of the slot kind `slot` on a turret: its angle, against the body's facing, is a part of the unit, which combat writes in place each tick, turning toward the bearing of the target of the attack under way by at most `turn_rate` degrees a second, and with none back toward 0 at half that rate, as `TurretAI` turns a turret to its target and its natural angle; an attack of the slot starts its windup only while the turret lies within `tolerance_deg` of its target's bearing, as `TurretAIAimTurretState::update` fires within 0.035 radians and OpenRA's `AttackTurreted.CanAttack` waits until a turret faces its target. Its owner's client predicts the angle, as it predicts the facing. The client turns the node its look names, `turret = "<node>"`, by the angle.
- **D9. A slow weapon delays its damage.** An `attack` action of no projectile takes `speed = "<m/s>"`: its hit lands ⌈`d` ÷ `s`⌉ ticks after its shot, for `d` the distance from its attacker's center to its target's in 3D and `s` its speed a tick, at once when `d` ÷ `s` is below 1, on the unit it was fired at wherever that unit then stands, as Zero Hour's `WeaponStore::setDelayedDamage` holds a weapon's damage; a target dead by then takes nothing. The hit is state of the match until it lands.
- **D10. A target lives by the one living test.** The choice of a target, for attacks, casts and splashes, reads `stats`' living test, so a unit at zero life that has not died yet, as `ctx.kill` leaves it until the next tick's deaths, is no target, as Zero Hour's `isEffectivelyDead` keeps such an object out of targeting and out of radius damage. It closes the issue log's item of combat.
- **D11. The import converts shells, splash, scatter, pitch, turrets and slow weapons.** A weapon whose `ProjectileObject` is an object of a `DumbProjectileBehavior` becomes an attack whose `delivery` names that object's unit type; the object becomes a projectile type, `speed` the weapon's `WeaponSpeed` (each weapon's own type when two weapons with other speeds fire one object), `width` twice its `GeometryMajorRadius`, `hits` the units of the tags `structure` and `wall` that `ProjectileCollidesWith` names, its `curve` the behavior's `FirstPercentIndent`, `FirstHeight`, `SecondPercentIndent` and `SecondHeight`, `follow` its `FlightPathAdjustDistPerSecond` and `life_ms` its `MaxLifespan`, 10,000 by default. The weapon's `PrimaryDamageRadius` becomes its `splash`, `affects` its `RadiusDamageAffects` and `spares_source` the absence of `SELF` there; its `ScatterRadius` and `ScatterRadiusVsInfantry` its `scatter`, the latter for the tag `infantry`; its `MinTargetPitch` and `MaxTargetPitch` its `pitch_deg`, when they are not the defaults; a weapon of no projectile and a `WeaponSpeed` below the default, of no laser, its `speed`. An object's `Turret` in its `AIUpdateInterface` becomes its type's `turret`, its `TurretTurnRate` and 2.005 degrees, the original's 0.035 radians, for its `ControlledWeaponSlots`' slot kind, `weapon`. An object's `GeometryHeight` becomes its type's `height`. A weapon is still dropped by every other cause of design 19's D6, and by a projectile of no `DumbProjectileBehavior`, a missile's, `Missile`, or a `SecondaryDamageRadius`, `Radius`.
- **D12. The client draws a projectile with its look.** The import converts each shell object's model, as it converts a unit's, into the projectile type's look; the client draws a projectile of a look with its model, at its point, its height included, turned along its flight, the way from its point of the tick before to its point now, and one of no look as the ball it draws now. A turreted unit's look names its turret node, the bone its draw module's `Turret` names in the model's first condition state, the Crusader's `Turret`, which the client turns by the unit's turret angle.

## Practice

What established engines do for each decision, read on 16 October 2026:

- **Ground in the simulation.** Spring and its fork Recoil test each projectile against the ground in their synced code, `CGround::GetHeightReal` and `CGround::LineGroundCol` in `ProjectileHandler::CheckGroundCollisions`; OpenRA's `Bullet` ends below the terrain by `Map.DistanceAboveTerrain`; Zero Hour's logic reads the terrain for a shell's curve. Each keeps the heights in the simulation (D1).
- **A flight in closed form.** OpenRA computes a bullet's point each tick from its two ends and its time, `WPos.LerpQuadratic(source, target, angle, ticks, length)`, so no rounding gathers; 0 A.D. computes a projectile's flight as it launches and keeps its graphics apart from the simulation (D3).
- **The projectile and its effect apart.** An OpenRA weapon holds a projectile and its warheads, which apply at its impact point: `SpreadDamageWarhead` with `Spread`, `Falloff`, `ValidRelationships`, allies included by default, and `AffectsParent`; a 0 A.D. attack holds a `Splash` of its `Shape`, `Range` and `FriendlyFire` (D5).
- **Contact along the way.** OpenRA sweeps a bullet's segment of each tick by its `Width` against blocking actors, and Recoil sweeps a ray against each unit's volume; Zero Hour tests its sphere at its point alone, which D4 keeps, so a shell meets what it meets in the original.
- **A turret that aims first.** OpenRA's `AttackTurreted.CanAttack` attacks only when a turret faces its target; Zero Hour's `TurretAIAimTurretState` fires within 0.035 radians (D8).

## Data

- **Map** (`map/<name>/heights.bin`, `map/<name>/map.toml`): the grid's samples and each cell's split; `[ground] high_cell = "40"`.
- **Unit types** (`data/units.toml`): `height`; `turret = { turn_rate, tolerance_deg, slot }`; for a shell, `projectile = { speed, width, hits, curve = { first, second, follow, life_ms } }`.
- **Actions** (`data/mode.toml`): `delivery = { projectile = "<shell>" }`, `splash`, `scatter`, `pitch_deg`, `speed`.
- **Mode** (`data/mode.toml`): the tags `structure`, `wall` and `infantry`, from the objects' `KindOf`.
- **Client** (`client/units.toml`): the shells' models, and `turret` in a turreted unit's look.

## Losses

What the slice does not hold against the original:

- **The launch point.** A shell starts at its attacker's center, where the original starts it at its gun's barrel bone (`Weapon::positionProjectileForLaunch`), a few meters ahead and above: its curve is a little longer, and its flight may take a point more.
- **The curve's rounding.** The original computes its points by forward differences in `f32`, whose rounding gathers along the curve; the engine computes each exactly. A point differs in its last bits, and a contact at a body's very edge may fall a tick apart.
- **The splash's measure.** The engine measures to a body's volume, the original to its bounding sphere, which holds its corners: a unit beside a long side of a box is reached from farther in the original.
- **The order of contacts.** A shell that meets two bodies in one tick hits the one of the lower stable id, where the original takes its contact list's order.
- **Effects.** No muzzle flash, explosion, recoil or sound; the impact shows in the target's life alone.
- **Upgrades and bonuses.** No `WeaponBonus`, no `Upgrade_ChinaUraniumShells`, no toxin shells: the Scorpion's death weapons wait for upgrades.
- **A shell's own life.** A shell has no life, where the original's has 100 and its armor, so nothing shoots one down.
- **Auto attack.** As in design 19, a tank attacks only what it is ordered to.

## Checks

- Geometry: the ground's height at each corner of a cell is its sample, on each side of a split the plane of that triangle, and past the edge the nearest point's; `highest_along` over a line of three coarse cells is the highest of their samples, the walk taking Bresenham's cells for a steep and a shallow line.
- Projectiles: a curve of a straight line and inner heights 0 has the points of the line, its count ⌈`L` ÷ `s`⌉ with `L` the chord; a curve of known control points has, at `t` = 0, ½ and 1, the values of the Bézier formula, exactly; its length is that of the split rule, at a curve that needs one split and one that needs none; a curve ends in the tick after its last point and as its life passes; a target that moves pulls the curve's end by `follow` a tick, at most; a sphere at a body's edge meets it, one a bit past it does not, and a body between two points is not met; a source is never met.
- Combat: a splash damages a unit within its radius and not one at it; spares or damages its source as data says; damages an ally of the relations it names; deals its target nothing twice; a scatter of radius 0 aims at the target, one above it at the point its two draws give; a pitch beyond its bound refuses the target; a turret of 180 degrees a second at 30 ticks turns 6 a tick, and its attack waits until it lies within its tolerance; an idle turret turns back at half its rate; a slow weapon's hit lands ⌈`d` ÷ `s`⌉ ticks after its shot, at once below one, and not on a target dead by then; a unit at zero life is no target of an attack, a cast or a splash.
- Import: a fixture's tank gun gives its attack with its delivery, splash, scatter and pitch; its shell, its projectile type with its curve, width, hits and life; its tank, its turret and height; a missile's weapon is dropped by `Missile`; a slow weapon gives its speed.
- Zero Hour check: the player trains a Crusader, and its shells destroy the bot's command center; the time of its first shot is the turret's turn, and the tick of its first detonation the curve's points; a shell aimed past a structure detonates on it. Its pins move with the slice, from a run on Linux and a run on macOS (design 12, D4).
