# Zero Hour projectiles

The first Zero Hour units that fire shells. The player builds a war factory, trains a Crusader, a Battlemaster or a Scorpion, and orders it to attack: its turret turns to the target, the gun fires a shell that flies a curve over the ground, and the shell detonates on the target, on a structure in its way, or where its curve ends, and damages every unit within its radius, the player's own among them. It builds on [Combat](04-capabilities/combat.md), [Zero Hour](12-zero-hour.md) and the [Client](02-engine-core.md#client), and takes the issue log's items of combat's living targets and of the damage a slow weapon delays. The facts it takes from are [Zero Hour research](../research/zero-hour.md#combat), its entry **Tank shells**; the practice of other engines it follows is in [Practice](#practice).

## Decisions

- **The sim's ground is the map's height grid.** The height grid, `map/<name>/heights.bin`, becomes data of the sim: its type moves from `client-files` to `units` as `HeightGrid`, beside `MapBook`, which holds the map's `Ground`, so its bytes enter the match's terms as the map's other data does; `units` holds it, not `geometry`, as its path is a package path of `values`, a crate of `geometry`'s layer. The ground's height at a point of the ground plane is the height of the cell's triangle that holds it, the plane through its three samples, computed from the samples in one step and rounded once ([Determinism core](09-determinism-core.md)); the map's `[ground] split`, `0_2` by default, names the one diagonal every cell splits on, from its corner 0 to 2 or from 1 to 3, its corners at the samples `(u, v)`, `(u + 1, v)`, `(u + 1, v + 1)` and `(u, v + 1)`. A sim's ground is its logic's, not what its client draws: Zero Hour's logic splits every cell on its own corners 0 to 2, the file's 1 to 3, whatever the terrain draws (`BaseHeightMapRenderObjClass::getHeightMapHeight`), so the import writes `1_3`, and the client keeps drawing the terrain file's own splits, its units standing on the drawn ground ([Client](02-engine-core.md#client)), as the original's do. A point off the grid takes the height of the grid's nearest point. A map with no height grid has the ground at height 0. On a `planar` map a unit's height in space is its ground's height at its point plus its height above the ground, the position's `y`. The map's `[ground] high = { cell, from, to }` gives the **coarse cells**, squares of `cell` meters that start at `from` and run toward `to` on each axis, as many as cover the span, each holding the highest of the samples its closed square holds, a square past the grid those of the grid's edge it clamps to, as Zero Hour's height past its grid is its clamped sample (`getClipHeight`), and as Zero Hour's partition cells of 40, from its world's corner over its playable area, hold the highest of the samples `calcHeights` steps over; with none, the coarse cells are the grid's own. A coarse cell is no smaller than the grid's, so each square holds a sample, and the cells are no more than the grid's samples, so their table is never larger than the grid it is of. `Ground::highest_along(a, b)` is the highest of the coarse cells a line of the ground plane crosses, from `a`'s cell to `b`'s by Bresenham's walk, as `PartitionManager::iterateCellsAlongLine` walks them, a cell off the coarse cells skipped, and none when it crosses none. The import writes `from` at the original's world corner, the engine's `[0, 0]`, and `to` at its playable area's far corner, so a row runs toward the engine's −z as the original's `y` grows, and each coarse cell has the original's edges.
- **A body has a height.** A unit type's `collision` section's `height`, in meters, at least 0 and 0 by default, makes its body a volume: a circle's body a cylinder, a box's a box, from its ground point up by `height`. Its **center** is its ground point raised by half its height, as Zero Hour's `getCenterPosition` raises an object's position by its geometry's half height. A shot aims at its target's center, and a splash measures to the volume.
- **A projectile may fly a curve.** A projectile type's `curve = { first = [<fraction>, <height>], second = [<fraction>, <height>], follow = "<m/s>", life_ms }` makes it fly a cubic Bézier curve in place of a line: its first point its launch, its last its target's center, or the point it aims at, and its two inner points, on the ground plane, at `first`'s and `second`'s fractions of the line from the first point to the last, and in height at `first`'s and `second`'s heights above the highest of the first point, the last, and the ground along the line, `Ground::highest_along`, as Zero Hour's `calcFlightPath` sets them. Its **points** are ⌈`L` ÷ `s`⌉, at least 1, for `s` its speed a tick and `L` the curve's length as `BezierSegment::getApproximateLength` measures it: half its chord plus half its control polygon, the curve split at its middle while the two differ by more than 1 m, each split point rounded once and each length to the nearest bit ([Determinism core](09-determinism-core.md)). A flight of more than 2²⁶ + 1 points flies in that many, faster than its speed, as each point's sum of its four control points by their weights then stays within 128 bits. In the tick after its launch it is at its first point, and in each tick after at the next, point `k` of `n` the curve's value at `t = k ÷ (n − 1)`, one point being the first, each coordinate computed from the four control points in one step and rounded once, so no tick's rounding carries into the next, as a falling projectile's ([Actions](04-capabilities/actions.md#deliveries)) and OpenRA's `WPos.LerpQuadratic` compute a flight; in the tick after its last point, or as its `life_ms` passes, it **ends** where it is. Before each step, a curve aimed at a living unit moves its last point toward that unit's center, by at most `follow` a second, when the center lies more than √0.1 m from it, and computes its inner points again with its point count kept, as `DumbProjectileBehavior::update` follows its victim. A curve's flight is state its type and its start define, so a snapshot holds its points' inputs, not its points. A curve takes no `homing`, `gravity`, `range`, `stop_on_hit` or `once_per_cast`, which a line's or a homing flight's only use; an action that delivers one aims at a unit or a point and launches one, and a weapon's delivery is a homing projectile or a curve.
- **A curve meets units by its point.** After the tick's movement, each curve projectile's sphere, of half its type's `width`, at its point, meets the volumes of the living units its `hits` filter selects, and its target's always, but never its source's: the first it meets, by stable id, is its hit, and it ends there, as Zero Hour's partition manager tests a shell's sphere against the geometry of what `ProjectileCollidesWith` names after the frame's updates (`PartitionContactList::processContactList`, `shouldProjectileCollideWith`). It tests no path between two points: a sphere that passes a body between two points meets it not, as a shell passes one in Zero Hour. A line projectile keeps its swept test.
- **An attack may splash.** An `attack` action takes `splash = { radius = "<m>", affects = "<relations>", spares_source }`, with a projectile `delivery` alone: at its projectile's end, by a hit, its last point or its life, it damages, by its `amount` and `damage_kind` as a hit would, each living unit of the relations `affects` names, as a filter names them, `enemies`, `hostiles`, `neutrals`, `allies` or `all`, whose volume lies nearer to the end's point than `radius`, in 3D, and its source too unless `spares_source`, `true` by default; the damage is its source's, as Zero Hour's `dealDamageInternal` counts a projectile's damage as its launcher's, and the units take it in stable id order. Its projectile's hit then deals nothing of its own, so the unit it hits takes the splash once. As OpenRA's weapons carry their effects in warheads, `SpreadDamageWarhead` beside its projectile, and 0 A.D.'s attacks a `Splash`, the splash is data of the weapon, and no script runs for it. The units within the radius come from the spatial index's query near the point.
- **An attack may scatter.** An `attack` action takes `scatter = { radius = "<m>", tags = { <tag> = "<m>", ... } }`, with a projectile `delivery` that flies a curve alone, as a homing one flies at no point: a shot at a target of a tag the table names adds that tag's radius, the largest of them when it holds several, to `radius`; when the sum is above 0, the shot aims at a point of the ground at a distance drawn from 0 to the sum and an angle drawn from 0 to a turn, in that order, from its target's ground point, on its own stream of the sim's random source, and its projectile follows no unit, as Zero Hour's `fireWeaponTemplate` scatters a weapon at infantry: it may miss, and detonate on the ground or on a unit its `hits` filter selects. The engine's random sequence is not Zero Hour's, so its shots fall elsewhere than the original's with the same seed.
- **An attack may bound its pitch.** An `attack` action takes `pitch_deg = [<low>, <high>]`: a target whose center lies below `low` or above `high` degrees as seen from its attacker's center, against the ground plane, is out of its reach, as `MinTargetPitch` and `MaxTargetPitch` bound a weapon's targets; the bound takes a least below 90° and a most above −90°, so the test by the sine of a pitch past it tells each pitch apart. The chase walks by the weapon's range alone, so a unit in range and out of its pitch bound stands and does not attack.
- **A unit type may have a turret.** A unit type's `turret = { turn_rate, tolerance_deg, slot }` puts its attacks of the slot kind `slot` on a turret: its angle, against the body's facing, is a part of the unit, which combat writes in place each tick, turning toward the bearing of the target of the attack under way by at most `turn_rate` degrees a second, and with none back toward 0 at half that rate, as `TurretAI` turns a turret to its target and its natural angle; an attack of the slot starts its windup only while the turret lies within `tolerance_deg` of its target's bearing, as `TurretAIAimTurretState::update` fires within 0.035 radians and OpenRA's `AttackTurreted.CanAttack` waits until a turret faces its target. Its owner's client predicts the angle, as it predicts the facing. The client turns the node its look names, `turret = "<node>"`, by the angle.
- **A slow weapon delays its damage.** An `attack` action of no projectile takes `speed = "<m/s>"`: its hit lands ⌈`d` ÷ `s`⌉ ticks after its shot, for `d` the distance from its attacker's center to its target's in 3D and `s` its speed a tick, at once when `d` ÷ `s` is below 1, on the unit it was fired at wherever that unit then stands, as Zero Hour's `WeaponStore::setDelayedDamage` holds a weapon's damage; a target dead by then takes nothing. The hit is state of the match until it lands.
- **A target lives by the one living test.** The choice of a target, for attacks, casts and splashes, reads `stats`' living test, so a unit at zero life that has not died yet, as `ctx.kill` leaves it until the next tick's deaths, is no target, as Zero Hour's `isEffectivelyDead` keeps such an object out of targeting and out of radius damage. It closes the issue log's item of combat.
- **The import converts shells, splash, scatter, pitch, turrets and slow weapons.** A weapon whose `ProjectileObject` is an object of a `DumbProjectileBehavior` becomes an attack whose `delivery` names that object's unit type; the object becomes a projectile type, `speed` the weapon's `WeaponSpeed` (each weapon's own type when two weapons with other speeds fire one object), `width` twice its `GeometryMajorRadius`, `hits` the units of the tags `structure` and `wall` that `ProjectileCollidesWith` names, its `curve` the behavior's `FirstPercentIndent`, `FirstHeight`, `SecondPercentIndent` and `SecondHeight`, `follow` its `FlightPathAdjustDistPerSecond` and `life_ms` its `MaxLifespan`, 10,000 by default. The weapon's `PrimaryDamageRadius` becomes its `splash`, `affects` its `RadiusDamageAffects` and `spares_source` the absence of `SELF` there; its `ScatterRadius` and `ScatterRadiusVsInfantry` its `scatter`, the latter for the tag `infantry`; its `MinTargetPitch` and `MaxTargetPitch` its `pitch_deg`, when they are not the defaults; a weapon of no projectile and a `WeaponSpeed` below the default, of no laser, its `speed`. An object's `Turret` in its `AIUpdateInterface` becomes its type's `turret`, its `TurretTurnRate` and 2.005 degrees, the original's 0.035 radians, for its `ControlledWeaponSlots`' slot kind, `weapon`. An object's `GeometryHeight` becomes its type's `height`, a sphere's its radius, as `GeometryInfo::set` takes it. A weapon is still dropped by every other cause of the import's weapon rule ([Zero Hour](12-zero-hour.md#the-importer)), and by a projectile of no `DumbProjectileBehavior`, a missile's, `Missile`, or a `SecondaryDamageRadius`, `Radius`.
- **The client draws a projectile with its look.** The import converts each shell object's model, as it converts a unit's, into the projectile type's look; the client draws a projectile of a look with its model, at its point, its height included, turned along its flight, the way from its point of the tick before to its point now, and one of no look as the ball it draws now. A turreted unit's look names its turret node, the bone its draw module's `Turret` names in the model's first condition state, the Crusader's `Turret`, which the client turns by the unit's turret angle.

## Practice

What established engines do for each decision, read on 16 October 2026:

- **Ground in the simulation.** Spring and its fork Recoil test each projectile against the ground in their synced code, `CGround::GetHeightReal` and `CGround::LineGroundCol` in `ProjectileHandler::CheckGroundCollisions`; OpenRA's `Bullet` ends below the terrain by `Map.DistanceAboveTerrain`; Zero Hour's logic reads the terrain for a shell's curve. Each keeps the heights in the simulation.
- **A flight in closed form.** OpenRA computes a bullet's point each tick from its two ends and its time, `WPos.LerpQuadratic(source, target, angle, ticks, length)`, so no rounding gathers; 0 A.D. computes a projectile's flight as it launches and keeps its graphics apart from the simulation.
- **The projectile and its effect apart.** An OpenRA weapon holds a projectile and its warheads, which apply at its impact point: `SpreadDamageWarhead` with `Spread`, `Falloff`, `ValidRelationships`, allies included by default, and `AffectsParent`; a 0 A.D. attack holds a `Splash` of its `Shape`, `Range` and `FriendlyFire`.
- **Contact along the way.** OpenRA sweeps a bullet's segment of each tick by its `Width` against blocking actors, and Recoil sweeps a ray against each unit's volume; Zero Hour tests its sphere at its point alone, which the curve's contact keeps, so a shell meets what it meets in the original.
- **A turret that aims first.** OpenRA's `AttackTurreted.CanAttack` attacks only when a turret faces its target; Zero Hour's `TurretAIAimTurretState` fires within 0.035 radians.

## Data

- **Map** (`map/<name>/heights.bin`, `map/<name>/map.toml`): the grid's samples; `[ground] split = "1_3"`, `high = { cell = "40", from = [0, 0], to = [<x>, <z>] }`, the playable area's far corner.
- **Unit types** (`data/units.toml`): `collision.height`; `turret = { turn_rate, tolerance_deg, slot }`; for a shell, `projectile = { speed, width, hits, curve = { first, second, follow, life_ms } }`.
- **Actions** (`data/mode.toml`): `delivery = { projectile = "<shell>" }`, `splash`, `scatter`, `pitch_deg`, `speed`.
- **Mode** (`data/mode.toml`): the tags `structure`, `wall` and `infantry`, from the objects' `KindOf`.
- **Client** (`client/units.toml`): the shells' models, and `turret` in a turreted unit's look.

## Losses

What the slice does not hold against the original:

- **The launch point.** A shell starts at its attacker's center, where the original starts it at its gun's barrel bone (`Weapon::positionProjectileForLaunch`), a few meters ahead and above: its curve is a little longer, and its flight may take a point more.
- **The curve's rounding.** The original computes its points by forward differences in `f32`, whose rounding gathers along the curve; the engine computes each exactly. A point differs in its last bits, and a contact at a body's very edge may fall a tick apart.
- **The splash's measure.** The engine measures to a body's volume, the original to its bounding sphere, which holds its corners: a unit beside a long side of a box is reached from farther in the original.
- **The ground's rounding.** The original computes a ground height in `f32`, from fractions of a cell it rounds first; the engine computes it from the samples exactly and rounds once, so a height differs in its last bits.
- **The grid's outer ring.** In the original, a point of the grid's outermost cells, or past them, takes one clamped sample (`getClipHeight`), where the engine takes the nearest point's height; the ring lies outside the playable area, where no unit stands and no shell flies.
- **A sphere's center.** The original's sphere stands around its position, its center there (`getZDeltaToCenterPosition`); the engine's volume rises from the ground point by the radius, its center half the radius above.
- **The order of contacts.** A shell that meets two bodies in one tick hits the one of the lower stable id, where the original takes its contact list's order.
- **Effects.** No muzzle flash, explosion, recoil or sound; the impact shows in the target's life alone.
- **Upgrades and bonuses.** No `WeaponBonus`, no `Upgrade_ChinaUraniumShells`, no toxin shells: the Scorpion's death weapons wait for upgrades.
- **A shell's own life.** A shell has no life, where the original's has 100 and its armor, so nothing shoots one down.
- **Auto attack.** As in [Zero Hour](12-zero-hour.md#losses), a tank attacks only what it is ordered to.

## Checks

- Units: the ground's height at each corner of a cell is its sample, on each side of each split the plane of that triangle, exactly, and past the edge the nearest point's; a coarse cell holds the highest sample of its closed square, from `from` toward `to` on each axis; `highest_along` over a line of three coarse cells is the highest of them, the walk taking Bresenham's cells for a steep and a shallow line and for a line toward `−x` and `−z`, and none off the coarse cells; a map with no grid is flat; a volume's distance to a point beside it, above it, below it and off its top edge, at the reach and a bit either side of it; a body's center halfway up its height above its ground, and a spatial map's at its position.
- Projectiles: a curve of a straight line and inner heights 0 has the points of the line, its count ⌈`L` ÷ `s`⌉ with `L` the chord; a curve of known control points has, at `t` = 0, ½ and 1, the values of the Bézier formula, exactly; its length is that of the split rule, at a curve that needs one split and one that needs none; a curve ends in the tick after its last point and as its life passes; a target that moves pulls the curve's end by `follow` a tick, at most; a sphere at a body's edge meets it, one a bit past it does not, and a body between two points is not met; a source is never met.
- Combat: a splash damages a unit within its radius and not one at it; spares or damages its source as data says; damages an ally of the relations it names; deals its target nothing twice; a scatter of radius 0 aims at the target, one above it at the point its two draws give; a pitch beyond its bound refuses the target; a turret of 180 degrees a second at 30 ticks turns 6 a tick, and its attack waits until it lies within its tolerance; an idle turret turns back at half its rate; a slow weapon's hit lands ⌈`d` ÷ `s`⌉ ticks after its shot, at once below one, and not on a target dead by then; a unit at zero life is no target of an attack, a cast or a splash.
- Import: a fixture's tank gun gives its attack with its delivery, splash, scatter and pitch; its shell, its projectile type with its curve, width, hits and life; its tank, its turret and height; a missile's weapon is dropped by `Missile`; a slow weapon gives its speed.
- Zero Hour check: the player trains a Crusader, and its shells destroy the bot's command center; the time of its first shot is the turret's turn, and the tick of its first detonation the curve's points; a shell aimed past a structure detonates on it. Its pins move with the slice, from a run on Linux and a run on macOS ([Zero Hour](12-zero-hour.md#decisions)).
