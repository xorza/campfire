# Campfire — Wide bodies, uses, the shop and gravity

Four mechanisms that design 04 gives and the engine lacks: bodies up to 2,048 m ([Space and map](04-capabilities/00-overview.md#space-and-map), [Zero Hour](12-zero-hour.md#decisions), D11), the `use` action kind ([Interaction](04-capabilities/interaction.md)), the client's shop and item keys ([Items](04-capabilities/items.md)), and projectiles that fall under `gravity` ([Actions](04-capabilities/actions.md#deliveries)). Each ends in something the user sees: a match with a body wider than a map's lane that walkers go around at the cost of a narrow one, a MOBA shrine a hero holds a use on, a shop the player buys from with item keys that cast, and a grenade that arcs. `PLAN.md` holds the steps.

## Decisions

### Wide bodies

- **D1. One bound for every body: 2,048 m.** `Shape::MAX_BOUND` rises from 64 m to 2,048 m, 2³⁵ bits of a `Num`, for a circle's radius and a box's farthest corner alike, and `BodyBox::MAX_DIAGONAL` from 126 m to 4,094 m, so a box's rounded corners stay within the bound as they do now. A walker keeps the same bound as a structure: no rule of collision asks which kind a body is, and D3 makes a wide walker cost only its own pairs.
- **D2. The exact math stays exact at the new bound, in wider integers where 128 bits do not hold a product.** Positions lie within ±2⁴⁴ bits, and a body's bound within 2³⁵, so a box's half edges, slab values, edge dots and squares stay below 2⁸⁴ and fit `i128` as they do. Three computations pass 128 bits, and change:
  - `Collider::reaching`: the square of `along · reach`, below 2¹⁴⁴, is a `U256`, and its quotient by the offset's square, rounded up, is below 2⁷² and a `u128` again, whose root rounds up as now. `U256` gains a division by a `u128` rounded up, by Knuth's algorithm D on 64-bit digits, two native divisions a digit, where a division a bit at a time takes 128 steps on every collision of two circles.
  - `outward`, the push of a circle out of a box: the bound `(along · radius)² · square`, below 2²¹⁴, is a `U256`. The fine root keeps as many bits of fraction as 128 bits leave beside the square, from 27 for the longest edge to 63 for the shortest way, and the estimate's product and quotient are exact in 256 bits, so the first `k` is the least or one below it at every size, where the 64 m bound kept 31 bits and an estimate fewer than 3 below.
  - `SquaredDistance`'s order: a point's distance to a path, up to 2¹⁸⁴ over 2⁹², and to a box's edge, up to 2¹⁶⁶ over 2⁷³, cross-multiply past 256 bits. Two distances compare by their cross products, each a `U256` times a `u128`, up to 384 bits, held as three 128-bit limbs, so any two compare exactly and none panics, at the cost of the two products a side that the 256-bit test took. `U256` gains that comparison, with no branch past 256 bits: a division there costs more, and the branch alone slows every comparison.

  Every bound that a comment states is derived again for 2³⁵, and a test at the bound runs each computation at its largest inputs: a 2,048 m walker pushed out of a 4,094 m box at its corner and along its edge, two 2,048 m circles on one spot and a bit apart, and the distances of D2's last bullet compared both ways.
- **D3. Each spatial index keeps its bodies in levels by size.** A uniform grid's cell suits its widest body, so mixed sizes cost every query the widest body's cell ([Ericson](https://realtimecollisiondetection.net/books/rtcd/), chapter 7): `BodyGrid` sizes its cells at twice the widest bound present, so one 1,117 m bridge of a Zero Hour map makes each cell over 2 km wide and every target, flight, area and aura query visit every body of the map; `BodyIndex` puts a static body in each bucket its box covers, buckets twice the widest walker's radius, so the same bridge covers some 200,000 buckets of a 0.35 m walker; the broadphase sizes its walkers' cells at twice the widest walker's radius. D11 said the bound "costs nothing until a wide body stands", and the bound rises for the maps where one stands. Each of the three becomes a hierarchical grid, as Mirtich's thesis proposes and Rapier's multi-SAP broadphase builds with its layers: cells double from one level to the next, from the narrowest body's base cell; a body goes into the least level whose cell is at least twice its bound, so it covers at most four cells; entries sort by level, row, column and stable id, so every run orders them alike; a query visits each level that holds a body, grown by that level's widest bound; the broadphase pairs a walker with the walkers of its own level's cells around it and of each coarser level's. A level with no body costs nothing, and from 0.35 m to 4,096 m there are at most 14 levels. A wide body then costs its own few cells, and each query one more level for each size class present. The collision benches gain the crowded scene with one 1,117 × 100 m box 2 m past it, which every walker's query of the static index meets, beside the scene without it: the 3v3's map, 96 × 136 m, holds no such box out of its lanes, and every unit stands within a map's bounds.

### Uses

- **D4. A use is its object's action, run in the user's use slot.** An object's type gives `use = { action, users }`: `action`, an action of its package of the `use` kind, and `users`, a filter of the units that may use it, by their relation to the object and their tags, `all` by default. A user's type gives `uses = { kind }`, the mode's slot kind of one action slot that holds the use under way, as an inventory names its slot kind; a type without it uses nothing, so a creep or a tower never does. The order `use = target` fills each of its units' use slot with the target's action at rank 1, ready and with no charges, unless it holds that action already, and runs on as the order of that slot at that unit: it walks in range, checks, winds up and resolves, and its channel is the held use. A unit with no use slot, or that `users` does not select, drops the order. This is Dota 2's design for outposts and lanterns, where each hero holds a hidden capture ability, and World of Warcraft's, where a player opens a locked object with an opening spell; here the object, not the user, gives the action, so each object brings its own outcome, and the whole pipeline of actions runs it, as an item's active runs in its inventory slot. Load checks: a `use` action has one rank, an instant delivery and no `cooldown_ms`, `charges`, `toggle` or `charge`, as the use slot is filled afresh; its group is `use`, so a tag that blocks `use` stops it; no type's `slots` and no choice put an action in a `uses` kind; a `use` section names a `use` action, and only a `use` section does.
- **D5. A channel may end on damage, and its ticks know their target.** `channel = { duration_ms, tick_ms, damage_interrupts }`: with `damage_interrupts = true`, damage of more than 0 to the unit interrupts the channel; damage applies in Resolve, so `on_interrupt` runs in the next tick's Hit, as a death's does. `on_channel_tick(ctx, unit, target)` takes the channel's target, so a held use can add to its object's progress. Shared progress stays the object's script state, as design 04 gives it.
- **D6. The MOBA's shrine.** A shrine stands in the middle of the jungle, on the hostile team, with no body to attack and no pools: any hero uses it, a held use of 3 s that damage interrupts; as it resolves, each hero of the user's team gains `shrine_blessing`, which heals over 10 s, and the shrine takes `shrine_spent` for 90 s, whose tag `spent` the use action's targeting excludes (`all:shrine:!spent`). A right click of the own avatar on a unit it may use orders the use, before an attack or a move; under the own avatar a bar fills over its channel. The scripted match has a hero use the shrine, and a second use cut by a hit.
- **What waits:** `enter`, garrisons and transports come with Zero Hour's containers (Stage 8); doors, dialogue and shared progress with the modes that use them.

### The shop

- **D7. Each player learns their own player resources.** A player's row of resources is a component of its player entity, of the kind `resources`, which reaches its owner, and the allies, enemies and spectators the mode's `[visibility]` adds ([Replication audiences](14-replication-audiences.md), D7). Its sim predicts no change to it: the row the server sent stands until the next.
- **D8. An item has a name.** `[items.<id>]` gains `name`, a message id of its package's locale, as an avatar's `name` is, and the load checks each locale holds it. The MOBA's items get their names in `en`.
- **D9. The client draws text with Bevy's UI.** The shop and the inventory need text, and the client has none. Bevy's own `bevy_ui` and `bevy_text`, with the embedded default font and Bevy's UI picking, add no crate outside Bevy; egui, through `bevy_egui`, is a new dependency and a second UI system. The client takes those Bevy features, named in the plan step as Bevy 0.19 lists them.
- **D10. The shop panel and the inventory bar.** P opens and closes the shop, as in League of Legends. The panel lists the shop's `items` in their order, each with its name and its price for the own avatar now, its cost less the components it holds, as the sim computes it, and the avatar's gold above; an item it cannot buy now, out of a shop and alive, too poor or with no room, is dimmed, and a click buys it, sending `buy`. The inventory bar, always shown, holds the avatar's inventory slots, each with its item's name, its stack or uses, its key and its cooldown; a right click on a slot sells it where the avatar can buy, and a slot dragged onto another swaps them. The client reads the shop and the prices through a public reader of `capabilities`, as `Learning` serves the HUD.
- **D11. Keys aim as their action takes.** 1 to 6 cast the actions of the inventory's slots, and Q, W, E and R the first four ability slots, each at the target its action's `targeting` takes: the unit under the cursor, the ground point under it, or none. The cast keys now send no point, so no action that aims at a point casts from the client, a ward's among them.

### Gravity

- **D12. A falling projectile's point is a closed form of its distance flown.** A line projectile with `gravity = g`, in m/s², flies the same distance `f` along its direction each tick as one without, and its point is `p₀ + d·f + ŷ·(lift·f − g·f² ÷ 2s²)`, for its launch point `p₀`, its unit direction `d`, its speed `s`. Each point is computed from `f` exactly, and rounded once to a `Num` for each coordinate, so no tick's rounding carries into the next, as a stepwise integration's would. On a `planar` map `d` lies on the ground plane and `lift = g·D ÷ 2s²`, for `D` the distance at which its line ends, so it rises and lands at its launch height where its line ends, its height at its peak `g·D² ÷ 8s²`: ranges, hits and its end stay those of the ground plane, and the height is its position's, which the client draws and a hit's `pos` carries. On a `spatial` map `d` is its aim and `lift` is 0, so it falls from its aim, as a Worms shell or a shooter's bullet does; each tick it flies the straight chord between its points at `f` and the next `f`, and hits what that chord meets, as Unreal's projectile movement and Source sweep one straight segment a step; it ends at its range, or where its point leaves the world's bound. Load checks: `gravity` is positive, and no homing projectile has one, as a homing one flies at its target.
- **Zero Hour does not use it.** Zero Hour's shells fly a cubic Bézier curve whose two inner points stand at heights above the highest terrain between the ends (`DumbProjectileBehavior`), not a fall; its import takes its own flight when its rules package needs it. Gravity serves Worms and the shooters of Stage 15, and wind comes with them.
