# Units that drive

The first Zero Hour units that move: a skirmish's start, its units driven by the player across an imported map round its cliffs, on navigation's generic grid and steering, and the client's controls to select and order them. The facts it takes from are [Zero Hour research](../research/zero-hour.md#movement).

## Decisions

- **D1. Zero Hour's units move by navigation's generic backends, with the original's numbers.** A unit type takes its normal locomotor's `Speed` as its `move_speed` and its `TurnRate` as its `turn_rate`, and its body's bounding circle as its radius; it plans on the grid's A* and follows by local steering. The original's pathfinder and locomotors, its acceleration, braking, turning while it drives and its locomotor kinds, stay the `navigation::zero_hour` backends of [Zero Hour](12-zero-hour.md#decisions)'s D3, which parity needs and its oracle checks, later. What the generic backends lose against the original is under [Losses](#losses).
- **D2. A walker faces the way it steps, turning at its turn rate.** The sim turns no unit now: `Facing` is its spawn's angle, sent once, and the client turns a model only as it spawns. `Facing` becomes a part navigation writes in place: each tick, a walker with a way to go turns toward its leg's way, from where the leg began, the waypoint it passed last or its place as its route or a detour began, to its next waypoint, so the way holds for a whole leg whatever each step rounds, and changes only as a leg begins; the shorter way round, counter-clockwise on a tie; one of the engine stat `turn_rate`, degrees a second, turns at most its rate over the tick rate, rounded once, and steps in that tick only when its facing then lies within 11.25° of the way, as Dota 2 lets a unit move within 0.2 radians, here the nearest angle of few bits; one of none faces the way at once. A forced move and collision turn no unit. The facing is sent and predicted as the position is (`SentPredicted`), and the client turns a unit's drawing as its facing changes, glided between ticks as its place is. The test content's walkers now face where they walk, so the goldens move once.
- **D3. The cliffs are walls.** The import grows the map's cliff cells by one ring, as the original's pathfinder grows them, merges them into rectangles row by row, and writes each as a wall of the map's `[navigation]`, on cells of 10 m, the original's pathfind cell. Tournament Desert's 48,689 grown cells are 286 rectangles, so no new map format is needed.
- **D4. Only walkers and standing structures have bodies.** As the original's pathfinder routes round a structure that is not mobile, not small and stands within a cell of the ground (`Pathfinder::classifyObjectFootprint`), an imported object has a body when it walks, a circle of its bounding radius, or when it is a structure that is not small, a box of twice its radii for `BOX` geometry, else a circle. Every other object has none and blocks nothing: an aircraft that only flies, a small structure, a mine, a fence. A body is its type's, so a structure the map places high above the ground blocks as one on it does.
- **D5. A map loads with overlapping obstacles, and checks the markers its mode spawns at.** The load refused a placed box that overlaps a wall or another placed static body, and checked that a walker can stand on every marker; under D3 and D4, 95 of the 116 shipped maps hold 2,089 structures over a grown cliff, 81 hold 1,474 overlapping pairs of structures, and 54 hold 2,175 waypoints too near a wall for their widest walker. The original has no such rule: obstacles that overlap all block. So placed static bodies may overlap walls and each other, as they block alike, while a placement at run time still refuses an overlap ([RTS foundations](11-rts-foundations.md#decisions), D8), and the load still refuses a box past the bounds. Mode data names the tags of the markers its units spawn at, `[navigation] spawn_tags`, and the load checks only those markers; the test modes name theirs, and every `Player_<n>_Start` of the shipped maps passes.
- **D6. A skirmish starts with the faction's start objects.** The import reads each player template's `StartingBuilding` and `StartingUnit`s, and tags each `Player_<n>_Start` and `Player_<n>_Rally` waypoint `start` beside `waypoint`; the rules package's mode script spawns, for the player at start position `n`, America's building at `Player_<n>_Start` and each starting unit at `Player_<n>_Rally`, or where the original starts its search, the building's point moved half its bounding sphere toward the original's `−y`, the engine's `+z`. Collision moves a unit clear of the building's box. A faction choice, the original's search for a free place (`PartitionManager::findPositionAround`), and the building's `PlacementViewAngle` wait: `spawn_unit` takes no angle, so the building stands unturned.
- **D7. The client controls a selection.** The player selects their own units: a left click on one selects it alone, a left drag selects each whose ground point lies in the drawn rectangle; a click or a drag that meets none of the player's units leaves the selection as it is. A right click orders every selected unit at once, as one group order ([Groups](04-capabilities/navigation.md#groups)): to attack an enemy under the cursor, else to move to the ground point. As the client plays, the selection is its avatar when it has one, so a MOBA plays as now; the ability and item keys act on the avatar as now. A ring on the ground under each selected unit, of its radius, shows the selection. The client's right click already orders, as StarCraft II's does.
- **D8. The ground under a unit is the drawn ground.** The client's data reads the map's terrain file, and its ground heights take each cell's diagonal from it, so a unit, a client object and the pointer's ray stand on the triangles the terrain draws; a map with no terrain file splits each cell from its first sample to the one across, and the view draws its plane. A point off the grid takes the height of the grid's nearest point, so the ground runs on level past its edge, with no step. Zero Hour's rule of a flat border ring goes, as the terrain draws every cell.
- **D9. At most four glTF models load at a time.** Bevy's glTF loader waits on a task scope for a model of two or more textures, and its thread runs the next queued load inside that wait (Bevy [#21954](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/issues/21954)), so the nesting grows with the models loading at once. The package asset source takes a permit of a semaphore of four before it opens a `.glb`, and its reader holds the permit until it drops: the asset server keeps the reader for the whole load (`load_with_settings_loader_and_reader`, bevy_asset 0.19.1), and a load that waits for a permit yields, so its thread runs other work instead of nesting it. The semaphore is `async-lock`'s, already in the build through Bevy.

## Data

- **Unit types** (`data/units.toml`), written by the import from each object's `Locomotor = SET_NORMAL`, its `KindOf` and its `Geometry`: `stats.move_speed` and `stats.turn_rate` from a locomotor whose `Surfaces` include `GROUND`, none for one that only flies or swims, which stands until its layer comes; `collision` as D4 gives it.
- **Maps** (`map/<name>/map.toml`): `[navigation] cell = 10` and the walls of D3; the start waypoints' markers tagged `start` as D6 gives them.
- **Mode** (`manifest.toml`, `data/mode.toml`): the capabilities `stats`, `orders` and `navigation`; `max_move_speed` the largest `move_speed` the import writes; `[navigation] spawn_tags = ["start"]`; `[params]` with `start_building`, `start_units`, a list, and `start_offset`, half the building's bounding sphere, of the faction the slice starts with, America.

## Losses

What the generic backends lose against the original, until the `navigation::zero_hour` backends come:

- **Driving.** A unit reaches its speed at once and stops at once, where the original accelerates and brakes; it turns in place before it drives, where the original turns while it drives, no slower than its `MinTurnSpeed`, and moves backwards when it may.
- **Paths.** A walker may stand on a cell whose centre lies at least its radius from every wall, where the original's unit needs whole cells clear round its centre cell (`Pathfinder::getRadiusAndCenter`): a way between two cliffs may be open in one and closed in the other. A fence blocks nothing, where the original routes round its line (`Pathfinder::classifyFence`); a structure placed more than a cell above the ground blocks, where the original's does not.
- **The start.** The command center stands unturned, and a starting unit where collision leaves it, not where the original's search places it.

## Checks

- Navigation: a walker of a turn rate turns at its rate and steps only within the angle, each angle and tick computed by hand; one of none faces its way at once; the goldens move once, for the test content's walkers now turn.
- Mode: a map of a box over a wall and of two boxes that overlap loads; a box past the bounds does not; a marker of a spawn tag in a wall's margin does not load, one of another tag does.
- Client: a click, a drag and a right click give the selection and the group order computed by hand; the ground heights of a fixture terrain of flipped cells on each triangle; the package source holds at most four `.glb` readers at a time, and a fifth read waits until one drops.
- Import: a fixture object's unit type, its speed, turn rate and body by D4's rule; a fixture map's cliffs grown, merged and written as walls; the player template's start objects as the mode's params, and the start waypoints' tags.
- The Zero Hour check: every imported map loads in the imported mode; Tournament Desert's walls, 286; the match starts with America's command center and dozer at `Player_1_Start`; the dozer, ordered to `Player_2_Start`, arrives within a bound of ticks, and never stands in a wall's cell.
