# Zero Hour production

The first Zero Hour units that are made: a skirmish's starting money, the trains and builds of every command set, a building's site placed as the order is given, a trained unit's exit and rally, and the client's command panel that orders them. It builds on [Production](04-capabilities/production.md), [RTS foundations](11-rts-foundations.md) and [Units that drive](17-units-that-drive.md). The facts it takes from are [Zero Hour research](../research/zero-hour.md#production).

## Decisions

- **D1. A build may start as it is ordered.** A `build` action gains `start`, `"arrival"`, the default, or `"order"`. An arrival build starts as [Construction](04-capabilities/production.md#construction) says, when its builder comes in range, as StarCraft II's SCV. An order build starts in the Act stage of the tick its order applies, wherever its builder stands: it passes its checks, pays, goes on cooldown and spawns its site there, and its order turns to that site, which its builder walks to and builds as any builder of a site does. So the money goes, and the site stands, as the player clicks, as Zero Hour's `DozerAIUpdate::construct` withdraws the cost and spawns the object before the dozer moves. A start that fails ends the order, as an arrival build's does.
- **D2. A producer may name where its units come out and go.** A unit type's `production` gains `exit` and `rally`, each `[x, z]` in meters in the producer's own frame, turned with it. A trained unit spawns at the free cell nearest the exit point, as a teleport finds its cell, in place of the cell nearest the rally point; with no rally point set, it walks to the default rally point, in place of standing. A rally point the player sets wins over the default. Zero Hour's import writes the `UnitCreatePoint` and `NaturalRallyPoint` of the exit module that names them, `DefaultProductionExitUpdate`, `QueueProductionExitUpdate` or `SupplyCenterProductionExitUpdate`; a `SpawnPointProductionExitUpdate` names bones of its model, which the sim does not hold, so its factory spawns as one of no exit.
- **D3. Every command set's trains and builds are imported.** Each object's `CommandSet` lists its buttons in order. A `UNIT_BUILD` button becomes a `train` action of its `Object`, a `DOZER_CONSTRUCT` button a `build` action, each once for the whole import, `train_<type>` and `build_<type>`, in the slot kind `command` of the object's type, in the set's order. Every object a converted button makes becomes a unit type, with its own command set, so each type the player can reach is a type of the package. A button is dropped, and the import reports it, when its object is no unit type the engine holds, a build of no box body or a train of no walker, or when its object's prerequisites hold a line the engine cannot hold: a science, 35 lines of the install, or several objects any one of which passes, 4 lines.
- **D4. The numbers are the original's.** A train's or a build's cost is its object's `BuildCost` in the player resource `money`, its time its object's `BuildTime` as design 12's times rule writes it, so the count of ticks is the original's `BuildTime × 30`, truncated (`ThingTemplate::calcTimeToBuild`). A `Prerequisites` line of one `Object` is a unit type of `requires.units`. A producer's `production.queue` is its `ProductionUpdate`'s `MaxQueueEntries`, 9 when it gives none. A build constructs with its `builder`, at a `range` of 10 m, the pathfinder's cell, as the dozer docks in the free cell beside the building nearest it (`findGoodBuildOrRepairPosition`), and a cancel refunds all, as `onDozerCancelConstruct` does.
- **D5. A skirmish starts with the game's default money.** The mode's player resource is `money`, and its params' `start_money` the `Value` of the `MultiplayerStartingMoneyChoice` marked `Default` in `multiplayer.ini`, 10,000; the rules package's mode script gives it to each player at the match's start.
- **D6. A train's or a build's name is the game's.** The load requires a `name` on each `train` and `build` action, as it does on an avatar's actions, so the client has a name for each button. The import reads the install's string table, `Data\<language>\generals.csf`, as `GameTextManager::parseCSF` reads it, and writes each converted button's `TextLabel` into the mode's locale, its hotkey marker `&` dropped.
- **D7. The client's command panel orders production.** The panel shows the first selected unit, in stable id order, that has a `train` or `build` action or is a site of the player's: a button for each such action, with its name, its cost and whether the player can afford it; a producer's queue, each entry's name and the head's progress, a click on an entry cancelling it; a site's progress, with a button that cancels it; and the player's amount of each of the mode's resources, Zero Hour's money alone. A train button orders that unit alone. A build button shows the building's look at the cursor's ground point, at its type's angle, drawn red where its box overlaps a wall or a body the client holds; a left click orders the build there, and a right click or Esc drops it. A right click on the ground or a unit, with a selection of producers and no walker, sets their rally point, as Zero Hour's does.

## Data

- **Unit types** (`data/units.toml`): `slots = { command = [...] }`, the converted buttons' actions in their set's order; `production = { queue, exit, rally }` for an object of a `ProductionUpdate`.
- **Actions** (`data/mode.toml`): `[actions.train_<type>]`, `kind = "train"`, `targeting = "none"`, `unit_type`, `cost = { money }`, `windup_ms`, `requires`; `[actions.build_<type>]`, `kind = "build"`, `targeting = "point"`, `start = "order"`, `construct = "builder"`, `range = "10"`, and the same cost, time and requirements; each with its `name`.
- **Mode** (`manifest.toml`, `data/mode.toml`): the capabilities `production`, and `abilities`, whose `slots` section a unit type's actions sit in; `resources = ["money"]`; `[[slots]] name = "command"`; `[params] start_money`.
- **Locale** (`locale/en.ftl`): each action's name, `action-train-<type>` and `action-build-<type>`, beside the mode's names.

## Losses

What the slice does not hold against the original:

- **Power.** A building's `EnergyProduction` is not imported, so a player short of power builds at full speed, where the original slows each build to as little as half (`calcTimeToBuild`, `MinLowEnergyProductionSpeed`).
- **A site's life.** A site has no life, as the imported mode has no combat yet, where the original's starts at 1 hit point and gains its maximum over its build time.
- **The ground under a building.** The original flattens the terrain under a building as it is placed (`TerrainLogic::flattenTerrain`); the import's heights do not change.
- **A site's look.** A site draws its building's look whole, where the original draws its scaffold and the building rising.
- **The panel.** The panel shows the first selected unit's actions alone, as text, with no button images, no hotkeys, no sell, no upgrades and no special powers; a building is placed at its type's `PlacementViewAngle`, which the player cannot turn by a drag.
- **Dropped buttons.** Of the install's buttons, 86 are not imported: 22 of a science prerequisite and 4 of an either-or one, so the generals' own units and buildings cannot be made; 36 trains of a unit that does not walk, the aircraft an airfield or a supply center makes, as the engine has no layer of the air yet; and 24 builds of no box body. The Zero Hour check pins each count.

## Checks

- Production: an order build pays and spawns its site in the tick its order applies, far from its builder, and its builder walks to it and builds it; a build that cannot start ends and pays nothing; a trained unit spawns at the cell nearest its producer's exit point, turned with the producer, and walks to its default rally point, or to a rally point its player set; the goldens do not move.
- Import: a fixture's command sets give their trains and builds in order, with their costs, times, queues, exits and requirements; the dropped buttons are reported by cause; a fixture string table gives each button's name; the starting money is the default choice's.
- Client: the panel's buttons, queue and progress for a fixture producer and site; a build preview's colour over a body the client holds and clear of it; a right click gives a rally order for a selection of producers and a move for one with a walker.
- The Zero Hour check: the counts of trains, builds and dropped buttons by cause; on Tournament Desert, the command center trains a dozer, the dozer builds a barracks, and the barracks trains a ranger, each paid from 10,000 and each done in its time in ticks.
