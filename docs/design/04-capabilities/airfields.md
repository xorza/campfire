# Airfields

A building's places and runways, and the sorties of the units it parks there. The facts are [Zero Hour's jets](../../research/zero-hour-jets.md).

## Sorties

- **An airfield is a capability.** The new capability `airfields` holds an airfield's places and runways, a jet's place, its taxi, takeoff, landing, reload, heal and return, as Zero Hour's `ParkingPlaceBehavior` and `JetAIUpdate` hold them apart from production and the locomotor. Refused: a part of production, which makes units and does not land them; a part of navigation, which walks units and does not refill their clips.
- **A jet takes off and lands on its runway.** A jet waits for its column's runway, taxis by forced dashes to its prep point, waits its takeoff pause, rolls from the runway's start and climbs out past its end; to land it waits for the runway, flies to the airfield's approach, lands from the end to the start and taxis to its place; one jet holds a runway at a time, as Zero Hour's jets do (decided 2026-10-27). Refused: OpenRA's vertical takeoff and landing on the place, under which a jet lifts off as a helicopter.
- **A jet takes off as a jet.** The sortie rolls its jet from a stop, gaining its `acceleration` a tick to its speed, toward the airfield's `approach = { height, past }`, `past` a share of the runway's length past its end, and lifts it toward the approach's height by its climb times `(1 − d / L)²`, `d` its distance to the runway's end and `L` the runway's length, through a flight goal of a height and a climb that the sortie sets each tick, as `JetTakeoffOrLandingState` sets its lift; it lets the runway go once past the end, flies home at its type's height, and lands from the approach, gliding at its least speed from the height it holds there to the runway's end, as only Zero Hour's landing state descends to it. Refused: a winged mover in navigation, as Zero Hour's `Locomotor::moveTowardsPositionWings` is its general mover of forces, mass and damping, which a jet needs only for this path; a roll at the jet's full speed and a climb past the runway's end.
- **A jet goes home.** A jet whose every clip that reloads at home is empty, or that stands idle in the air for its sortie's idle time, returns to its airfield, lands, reloads and heals at its airfield's heal a second while it rests there, as `JetAIUpdate::update` returns it; one whose airfield is gone takes the nearest airfield of its team, an ally's among them, that stands built and unsold with a free place, as `findSuitableAirfield` takes one of `ALLOW_ALLIES` and refuses one under construction or sold, and with none circles where it stands, losing its sortie's share of its life a second, as `JetOrHeliCirclingDeadAirfieldState` does; an airfield that dies kills the jets it holds, as `ParkingPlaceBehavior::killAllParkedUnits` does. The heal and the loss go through combat's pass as its `Upkeep`. Refused: OpenRA's flight idle with no damage, under which a jet with no airfield circles for ever; a heal or a loss written to the life pool, past combat's hooks and deaths.
- **Airfields asks the owners of what it changes.** A jet taken in by another airfield takes its place through production's `Rehome`, which holds the place, holds its door open and makes the jet that producer's, and a jet's clips refill at home through combat's `ClipShots::reload_at_home`, which knows which clips refill there; airfields writes neither's parts and repeats neither's rule. Refused: the parts moved into `units` as a contract, which renamed four saved parts for no change of behaviour; events that production and combat observe, which moved the writes out of the sortie pass's order (decided 2026-10-29).
- **An order waits for the air.** An order takes a jet that still holds a shot off its way home; one given from its runway's approach to its roll waits, held by the tag `airfield_held`, and runs after its reload; a jet sent home empty drops its attack and takes none up, as Zero Hour's `aiDoCommand` holds a command while a jet lands or takes off and resumes only a guard or a hunt. Refused: a stored command run again after every reload, which Zero Hour does not do for an attack.
