# Navigation

## Mechanism

- **Routes, paths, forced movement and collision on layers.** Navigation moves units on the layers of the core's space ([Space and map](00-overview.md#space-and-map)).
- **Backends behind one interface.** Queries take world positions, a route is a list of waypoints and obstacles are shapes, so every movement backend works with every pathfinding backend: grid A* and local steering now, a navmesh and flow fields later, and an imported game's own pathfinder and locomotors as backends for its parity.
- **Each layer is its own world.** Each layer has its own pathing grid, static bodies and collision, and a unit's layer is a tag, so a filter selects air or ground.

## Movement

- **Three kinds of avoidance, as Dota 2's.** A long route around what never moves, a short route around units in the way, and collision as the last guard, all exact fixed point, in stable-id order, under a fixed work limit a tick.
- **Regions as 0 A.D.'s hierarchical pathfinder.** For each kind of walker, chunks of 64 × 64 cells split into regions numbered as reachable sets, so an unreachable goal turns into the nearest reachable cell before any search spreads, as 0 A.D.'s `MakeGoalReachable`; a change of static bodies builds again only the chunks it changed.
- **One work limit for every route.** Long and short routes share a limit a tick, as many units of work as the grid has cells; long routes go first, and the plan that meets the limit finishes while the rest wait, so the worst tick stays a small share of the budget.
- **A walker faces its leg and turns at its turn rate.** Each tick a walker turns toward its leg's way the shorter way round, at most its `turn_rate` over the tick rate, and steps only when it faces within 11.25° of the way, as Dota 2 lets a unit move within 0.2 radians; the way holds for a whole leg whatever each step rounds. A forced move and collision turn no unit.
- **Walks through a contract of `units`.** A capability asks for a walk by `Destination`, never by navigation's own state, and reads `ArrivedShort` to know a walk ended short, so production and abilities walk units with no dependency on navigation.
- **A route ends short, not never.** A unit that arrives short of its goal drops its destination and keeps its route, so the same goal plans nothing again until a static body goes.
- **A route in sight is a line.** A route whose goal the walker may stand on and sees, by the exact test of the static bodies, the walls and the priced cells a route's smoothing makes, is the straight line to it, before any search of cells, as a route's smoothing keeps such a line. Refused: the cells alone, under which a goal seen past two posts that meet at a corner, which no step of cells passes, is out of reach, and every route in sight pays a search; the Zero Hour check's bases play ends in tick 11,012 with 323 stalls with the line, against 11,801 and 360 without ([Crowd movement](../../research/crowd-movement.md)).
- **A stuck walker plans again, then stops.** A walker stuck for 1 s asks a new long route from where it stands, a detour it does not walk freeing it of nothing (A walker progresses only when it comes nearer its waypoint), as Zero Hour's `AIUpdate` plans a path again for a unit blocked and stuck and 0 A.D.'s `UnitMotion` asks a long path once its short paths fail three times; after 5 such asks with no waypoint passed between, about 5 s, it drops its goal and its route and stops where it stands, as Spring cancels the move of a unit that does not move, so an order to the same goal plans again. Refused: a route kept for good, which held a Zero Hour defender 5,509 ticks; an ask at the 150 ms that makes friends step aside, whose asks from a lane's crowd of creeps held a 3v3 hero below its level; ending short with the memory of an arrival short, which refuses the same goal until the static bodies change, though units that blocked it move on; passing through units while it plans again, as Zero Hour does, which leaves units inside each other (decided 2026-10-24).
- **A walker progresses only when it comes nearer its waypoint.** A tick counts toward being stuck unless the walker stands at least half its step nearer its waypoint than it ever stood, or passed a waypoint; a waypoint taken fresh, as a walk starts or past a waypoint, counts nothing, and a detour, a short route or a new route to a waypoint of their own in place of one not passed counts on. As 0 A.D.'s `CCmpUnitMotion` counts a failed movement each turn its move is obstructed or a push takes it away from where it moves, never escalates one that moves a little, and keeps the count across new paths. Refused: a move of half a step in any way, under which a hacker pushed back each tick by a crawler's personal space stood 0.77 m short of its goal for 6,240 ticks; a count a detour resets, under which a Red Guard took a detour it could not walk every 5 ticks and never asked a new route; a move of half a step along its leg each tick, under which a walker slowed in a crowd counted as stuck and the bases play's stalls rose from 332 to 1,065 ([Crowd movement](../../research/crowd-movement.md)).
- **A walker stuck by a crowded goal has arrived.** A walker stuck where its route's last waypoint and itself lie within the personal space a unit that stands shares with it, as collision measures it, has arrived there, not short, as near as collision lets it come, as the A* Pathfinding Project stops a unit at a crowded destination and Zero Hour's locomotor ends a move within its close-enough distance. Refused: arrival within its radius of the waypoint, under which a dozer stopped 41 m short of its building site and the attack play never ended; 5 asks of 1 s each, which leave it jittering by its goal for 5 s ([Crowd movement](../../research/crowd-movement.md)).
- **Head-on walkers pass on the right.** A walker kept back for 150 ms treats those it touches as offset to its left, so two that meet head on pass.
- **A client predicts its own routes** on the same grid, with no correction.

## Groups

- **One command, one route.** A move of many units is one group, and the group plans one long route for each layer, for its widest walker, which each unit joins at the last waypoint its own straight line reaches; a group costs one search a layer, about a tenth of its units one by one. A route that stops short of the group's goal serves only a unit of the walker it was searched for that has that goal: a narrower one, or one of its own goal, plans its own, from the tick's route budget, as one that sees no waypoint does.
- **Formation only away from the group.** Each unit keeps its offset from the group's center when the goal lies outside their box, and goes to the goal itself when it lies inside, as StarCraft II keeps a selection's formation only for a move away from it; a spawn group's wave shares its route alike.

## Crowds

- **A unit moves by its kind of motion.** A unit type's navigation says how it moves: `step`, as infantry, which may take a push in any direction, or `drive`, as a vehicle, which moves only along its facing and yields by driving; an import gives each type its kind ([Zero Hour](../09-zero-hour.md#the-importer)). Refused: a kind read from the turn rate, as a soldier turns at a rate too; one motion for every unit, under which a pushed tank slides sideways ([Crowd movement](../../research/crowd-movement.md)).
- **A unit has a weight.** A unit type's navigation gives it a weight, and of two units that part, each moves by the other's weight over its own, as 0 A.D.'s pushing does, so a soldier gives way to a tank and a tank barely to a soldier. Refused: a fixed order of classes, which a game of other classes cannot say.
- **Who may move whom is the game's.** A mode's navigation says which units a walker may move: its own team's, which holds its allies, as a team holds every player allied in it, or none, as Dota 2's units block one another and an enemy blocks everywhere. A unit that attacks, works, uses an ability or holds its position is never moved, as Zero Hour's `moveAllies` leaves the busy and StarCraft II's hold position stays. Refused: rules for one game in the capability ([Games](games.md)); kinds that pass through each other, as Zero Hour's infantry walks through its own (`AIPathfind.cpp`, `moveAllies`), as no two bodies overlap here and a unit that steps gives way to another by a push (decided 2026-10-24).
- **A route prices the units on it.** The long route's search adds a cost to each cell a unit of the walker's team that stands holds, three diagonal steps for one the walker may move and twice that for one it may not, as Zero Hour's pathfinder prices allied cells at three diagonal steps; so a route goes round a crowd when the way round costs less, and through it when not, and its smoothing cuts no line across a priced cell. A chase whose target moves keeps its route by a straight line only where that line crosses no priced cell either, else it plans again: the Zero Hour check's bases play stalled 445 times so, against 627 with a line past walls and static bodies alone (2026-10-24). An enemy costs nothing, as Zero Hour prices only allies: a walker fights it or meets it. The costs live in counts per walker kind and team beside the pathing grid, written for the units whose place or standing changed, so a search reads them as it reads the grid. Refused: units hidden from the long route, so no route goes round a crowd; enemies priced, under which an attack's routes go round the units it should fight; a flow field or a density field of the whole map each tick, which costs every tick what a change costs once; units as walls, which turns a crowd into a maze; Zero Hour's cost near a moving ally, as a walker moves on before the route reaches it.
- **A unit that steps yields by a push.** Units that `step` part before they touch: within a personal space of 5/4 of each body's radius, each pair of which one walks takes a push apart of a quarter of the intrusion a tick, each side the other may move, by weight, in fixed point, as 0 A.D.'s pushing does in its lockstep simulation; a push into a static body is dropped, and two that stand take none, so a crowd at rest settles. Refused: parting only bodies that overlap, which makes a crowd judder; 0 A.D.'s pressure, a part on each unit that damps one pushed again and again, as no push comes between units at rest; ORCA's velocity obstacles, which take any velocity a unit may want and solve a floating linear program each tick, and need an extension for a unit that turns; DetourCrowd's sampled velocities, which score dozens of samples per unit per tick.
- **A unit that drives yields by driving.** A unit that `drives` takes no push, and collision moves it only along its facing: a walker it is in the way of gives it a short move off the walker's route, which it turns to and drives, then stands there idle, as Zero Hour's `aiMoveAwayFromUnit` puts a unit in its `AI_MOVE_OUT_OF_THE_WAY` state and OpenRA's `Mobile` nudges an idle actor. Refused: a push sideways, which reads as a tank that slides; a vehicle that never yields, which blocks a factory's exit.
- **A new unit leaves its exit.** A unit that leaves its producer passes through its own team's units, with no push and no short route round them, until it overlaps none or stands, as Zero Hour's exit path may path through units; the idle units on its way step aside, and at its rally point it packs with the units that arrived there. Refused: the exit as any walk, which stalls against the last unit trained; Zero Hour's `moveAlliesAwayFromDestination`, which clears the rally point for each new unit and churns the crowd there.
- **A crowded goal ends the walk.** A walker that touches an idle unit of its team that arrived at the same goal, nearer the goal than it, has arrived too, as the A* Pathfinding Project stops a unit whose crowded goal it cannot reach; so a group sent to one point packs round it and none circles it, and no walker steps such a unit aside. A unit keeps the goal it arrived at until it walks again. Refused: each unit to its exact point, under which the last ones push the first or circle; a formation's slots, which a move away from the group keeps already (Formation only away from the group).

## Waypoint paths

- **Paths as lanes and patrols.** A unit walks a map's path in its spawn's direction while it has no other order, routing round the structures between waypoints; past the last waypoint it stays, goes on to the first, or turns back, as the path's `walk` says.
- **A path says how it is walked.** The walk is the path's map data, as a Zero Hour map's waypoint path holds its own links and its `biDirectional` flag, so a lane and a patrol differ by their paths. Refused: a walk for each walker, which each spawn call and placement would repeat.
- **Navigation follows paths.** Path walkers walk in navigation, before orders' chase, so a path's walk, its end and its hook are one crate's; orders only tells a walker to leave its path or rejoin it.
- **A path's end runs a hook.** A walker that reaches its last waypoint runs `on_path_end`, queued on the server by stable id in its tick; a predicting client walks alike and runs no hook.

## Forced movement

- **Dashes and knock backs pass through units,** as in League of Legends and Dota 2, and end before a static body; a dash crosses walls, as League of Legends' dashes do, and lands on the nearest cell it may stand in.
- **A dash follows its target,** as Lee Sin follows his mark, until their bodies touch.
- **Only a teleport disjoints,** as Blink does in Dota 2; a dash and a knock back do not, as Force Staff does not.
- **A forced move interrupts and replaces.** A moved unit takes no step and starts no action; a new forced move replaces the one under way.
- **A forced move is state,** and a client continues one it learned of.

## Map checks

- **A map that loads can be walked at its start.** The load checks, for each layer's widest walker, that each path's waypoints reach each other and that each marker of the mode's spawn tags is a place it may stand, using the regions the match uses.
- **The walk check builds its own grid.** The load builds a grid for each layer's widest walker with the placed bodies and drops it; the match builds one for every walker kind, which its static tracking fills with the match's own bodies, as Zero Hour builds its pathfinder at each map load (`Pathfinder::newMap`). Refused: one grid kept as a book and cloned by each match, which moves the check from the package load to the match start, so a map that cannot be walked loads.
- **Placed obstacles may overlap.** Placed static bodies may overlap walls and each other, as they block alike: Zero Hour's maps hold thousands of such overlaps and the original has no rule against them. A placement at run time still refuses an overlap ([Production](production.md#placement)).
- **Walker classes and a route budget, planned.** A mode declares its walker classes, so the clearance and region sets are one for each class, not one for each distinct radius, once a Zero Hour import's count of distinct walker radii is measured; and a route budget for each tick takes the place of the map's cell count as the route work limit.

## Room

- **Every body is tested for room.** `spawn_unit` refuses a unit whose body overlaps a wall, a static body, or any body the same call spawns, a circle and a box alike, a walker's among them ([Test matches](../../research/test-matches.md)). Refused: the test of boxes alone, under which a cylinder building spawns over another and a call's walkers spawn on one point.
- **A script finds room near a point.** `room_near(type, position, gap, steps)` gives the nearest point within `steps` gaps of `position`, at most 128, on a grid of `gap` meters round it, by distance and then by angle from `+x` as a facing turns, each compared exactly, where a unit of `type` has room as `spawn_unit` tests it, `gap` meters clear of each body and wall, a box grown at each side; none when no point within its steps has room, as StarCraft II's search takes its range. It takes the nearest point, as StarCraft II's `UnitTypePlacementFromPoint` and Age of Empires II's `find_closest` do, so a base stands packed round its start and the same in every match; its range counts grid steps, as a script call takes one number of meters at most, so its bound is exact. Refused: a cell drawn at random from an annulus, as OpenRA's support actors take, under which a test does not repeat; positions in data for each map and faction, made again for each.
- **A spawn stands where it can walk.** The pathing grid takes a box's body as the box spawns, whether it overlaps a walker or not, and a unit a producer with neither an exit nor a rally point spawns stands at the center of the open cell nearest the producer, as a trained unit stands at the free cell nearest its exit and a teleport finds its cell ([Production](production.md#train-queues)). Refused: a box that joins the grid from the next tick, under which a supply center's truck, sent as the center spawned, took the cell under the center as open, and collision pushed it out into a pocket of its base whose ways out are narrower than its body, which it never left; the producer's own place; a test of `room_near`'s places against the grid, as the truck was a spawn of production, and `room_near` keeps a gap clear of each body ([Crowd movement](../../research/crowd-movement.md)).

## Collision

- **Collision reads the walkers alone.** The Collide stage reads only the units that can walk into its colliders, and a contact of a walker with a static body keeps that body as the static index holds it, as the static bodies never move, so the stage costs the walkers, not every body. Refused: every body read into the colliders each tick, and a search of them for the body each static contact names.
- **Collision leaves no overlap.** After the pushes and the steps, each overlapping pair on one layer parts along the line between them, each coordinate rounded away from zero, in passes in stable-id order until one parts none, 8 at most, the unit of less weight taking more and a unit that drives only along its facing but on the last pass; then each walker from the static bodies it overlaps once more, so no later push leaves it in one. A walker a static body parted holds against the other walkers for the rest of the tick, so a pair beside a wall parts at once. A pair still deeper than a 64th of its reach is counted: a chain shrinks its overlaps with each pass and never ends them, and two walkers wedged between two static bodies need more than 4 passes. The proving match and each crowd case fail on any. Refused: one pass, which leaves the overlaps a crowd chains; passes until none overlaps, which a chain makes unbounded; Zero Hour's two seconds of no collision for a unit asked twice, which lets bodies cross.
- **A standing body blocks.** A walker that runs into a unit that stands takes the whole overlap, so a standing unit's body blocks; a unit that cannot walk leaves all of it to the other. An idle friend on a walker's way steps aside (An idle friend steps aside).
- **An idle friend steps aside.** A walker makes each unit its mode lets it move that stands idle on the next stretch of its way, as far as its short route's window reaches, with no destination, nothing under way, no target, no work and no tag that holds it, and that did not arrive at the walker's own goal, step off its way, on the side it stands, or to the walker's left from the way itself, as walkers pass on the right, until it clears the walker's body, at the nearest place it may stand there, before the walker reaches it; a unit that steps aside clears its own way in turn, 2 deep at most to a side (A friend yields through the friends that yield), as Zero Hour's `moveAllies` stops past a depth of 2, by a part on each unit that holds how deep its step aside is, as the spread runs over ticks here, not in one call; a builder at its build stays, as production shows in `AtWork`, and so does an enemy. It is the server's: a predicting client takes the step from the server. Zero Hour's `AIUpdateInterface` tells an idle unit in the way to `aiMoveAwayFromUnit`, a busy one not, and its pathfinder moves allies off a new path; OpenRA's `Mobile` queues a `Nudge` on an idle friendly actor that blocks a move ([Test matches](../../research/test-matches.md)). Refused: a standing body that blocks for good, under which the trained units at a rally hold back every walker behind them; a spread of trained units round their rally alone, which leaves every other idle crowd in the way; a step aside only for a walker stuck 150 ms, under which a column halts at each idle soldier; the friend it touches alone, which leaves the next one on the way; no bound on the depth, under which the steps aside round a rally point move the crowd that packed there ([Crowd movement](../../research/crowd-movement.md)).
- **A friend yields to the nearest place clear of the way.** A friend in the way takes its step off the walker's first stretch when no static body blocks it there and its body clears the walker's route ahead as far as the short route's window reaches, as the units it steps into step aside in turn; else it searches outward from where it stands, over the cells it may stand in that no other unit that stands holds but the friends that yield (A friend yields through the friends that yield), for the nearest whose center clears that route, as Zero Hour's `getMoveAwayFromPath` floods outward from the ally for the first free cell off the mover's path. Refused: the step off the first stretch alone, which a turn of the route walks into again, as a dozer's turn north did; a search over cells other units hold, which sent a trained unit into a crowd's member at its rally ([Crowd movement](../../research/crowd-movement.md)).
- **A friend yields through the friends that yield.** A friend's search for a place clear of the walker's way reaches as far as the walker's passage, never through the walker, and passes through the idle friends the walker may move, which step aside in turn for the friend's walk: one that steps ahead along the walker's way keeps the walker's depth, so a column chains as far as it reaches, and one that steps to a side goes a step deeper, 2 deep at most, as Zero Hour's `moveAllies` moves the allies on an ally's way, its search off the path bound by no window and through units when none is free, and OpenRA's nudge moves an idle neighbour. Refused: friends as blockers of the search, under which a dozer column was hemmed in and the dozer behind it arrived short; a chain 2 deep ahead too, which stops that column; the short route's window, under which a column in a corridor to open ground found no place; a search through the walker, under which a friend took a place behind it and the two pressed on each other for 6 asks; a push of idle friends by the walker, as StarCraft II pushes idle units, which a unit that drives does not take (A unit that drives yields by driving) ([Crowd movement](../../research/crowd-movement.md)).
- **A walker walks through the friends it moves aside.** A walk that may move the idle friends on its way, of its own or a step aside, steers through them, each stepping aside for it, as Zero Hour's pathfinder moves the allies off a path that goes through them (`moveAllies`), and into the crowd that arrived at its own goal, which it stops against as it touches one (A crowded goal ends the walk); a walk too deep to move them steers round them. Refused: a short route round them, under which a friend whose place lay on another idle friend ended its step aside short in its own cell and was asked again every 4 ticks for good, and a walker whose goal lay past a friend it was moving aside arrived short in that tick; a short route round the crowd at its goal, which cannot end on the goal, under which the attack play's walkers stalled 1,228 times and it never ended; a step aside alone through them, which keeps the second: the Zero Hour check's bases play ends in tick 12,082 with 371 stalls and its attack play in tick 18,630 with 259, against 9,981 and 241 and 24,741 and 354 for steps aside alone (decided 2026-10-25).
- **A friend that cannot yield is firm, and a stuck walker's route walls it.** A friend whose search finds no place is hemmed in, firm until it walks, by a part, so routes price it as one the walker may not move and the route a stuck walker asks treats the cells it holds as walls, as OpenRA plans again with every actor blocking after its nudge; with no way past it, the walker arrives short. A step aside that ends short where it began, within half its step, found no place it reaches, and is firm too. Refused: every step aside that ends short firm, under which a walled block's friends that had moved on were firm and its walker went round the walls; the friend priced as movable again, under which each new route ran through it and a dozer stood until its player ordered another goal; every firm unit walled, under which a 3v3 hero stuck behind its fighting creeps farmed below level 6; walls for every standing unit on the first route, which turns a crowd into a maze; a detour that goes as near as it can toward a waypoint within a unit's reach, under which a walker against a busy friend moved back and forth each tick, never stuck and never stopping ([Crowd movement](../../research/crowd-movement.md)).
- **A stuck walker's route walls the units of other teams that stand.** The route a stuck walker asks treats the cells a unit of another team that stands holds as walls, as it does those a friend hemmed in holds, as OpenRA plans a blocked move again with every actor blocking and Zero Hour's pathfinder blocks a non-ally that stands and cannot be crushed (`enemyFixed`); its first route still prices them at nothing, so an attack goes to the units it fights. Refused: units of other teams priced on every route, which the design refused before, as an attack's route then goes round the units it should fight; neutrals alone, which leaves the same jam against an enemy that stands; friends that are busy walled, under which a 3v3 hero farmed below level 6; no walls, under which China's supply trucks planned through a civilian taxi for 16,890 ticks ([Crowd movement](../../research/crowd-movement.md)).
- **A walker never stays in a static body.** A walker its static contacts leave overlapping a static body, as one wedged between two closer than its body is wide, which each contact pushes into the other, stands at the nearest place it may stand on the pathing grid, as a static body that spawns on a walker moves it. Refused: the contacts alone, under which such a walker swings between the bodies each tick and stands in blocked cells for good ([Test matches](../../research/test-matches.md)).
- **A route leaves a blocked start.** A long route from a cell no open cell borders, as a walker pushed into a gap narrower than its clearance stands in, leaves from the open cell nearest it, as one beside it does. Refused: a route of the walker's own place, which it never walks out of.
- **Gatherers in their loop do not collide** ([Production](production.md#gathering)).
