# Command panel

A slice's proposal. At its end, a player of a Zero Hour skirmish gives commands from a panel of the game's own button images, laid out as the game lays them, presses each button by its key, and sells a building from it. Its plan is `.notes/PLAN.md`'s Command panel; when the slice ends, its lasting decisions fold into `02-engine-core.md` for the panel, `04-capabilities/production.md` for the sale and `09-zero-hour.md` for what the import converts and loses, and this document goes. Its facts are [Zero Hour's command panel](../research/zero-hour-command-panel.md).

## Decisions

- **A button's look is its action's.** An action's look in the client files may name its icon, a rectangle of a texture of the package, and its key, so any game's panel draws its trains, builds and researches as images, each pressed by its key; an action of no icon keeps its text. Refused: images and keys in the sim's action data, which the server never reads.
- **An icon is a rectangle of a texture.** An icon names its texture and its rectangle in pixels, and the client draws it through Bevy UI's image node and its rectangle, so a game's atlas converts once, as its other textures do. Refused: each icon cut into a texture of its own at the import, hundreds of files for what one atlas holds.
- **A look places its buttons.** A unit type's look may give the place of each of its panel's buttons, an action of its slots or a command, in a grid whose columns, rows and order of places the HUD file gives; a look of no places lays its actions in slot order. Zero Hour's import writes each command set's places and a grid of 7 columns of 2, its places down each column, as `ControlBar.wnd` lays them. Refused: places in the sim's slots, which hold no layout; a fixed grid of one game in the client.
- **The panel's commands are the engine's orders.** A look may place a command, `sell`, `stop`, `attack_move` or `rally`, each its icon and key in the HUD file: a sale and a stop order at once, an attack move and a rally take a point the next left click picks, as the build preview takes its place. Refused: a command for each game's button, as Zero Hour's guard and its powers, which the engine does not hold yet.
- **A key presses a shown button.** A key with no modifier presses the shown button of that key, as Zero Hour's `HotKeyManager` presses its window of the key, so a key is a button's own mnemonic wherever its place; one the player cannot press does nothing. Refused: keys by place, as OpenRA's `ProductionSlot` hotkeys, under which a key presses another button as the list changes; Zero Hour's game keys by letter.
- **A tooltip tells what an icon hides.** Hovering a button shows its name, its key, its cost and its time, as Zero Hour's tooltip shows its label; a button its player cannot pay stays dimmed, and the queue shows each entry's icon, the head's progress as a bar over it. Refused: Zero Hour's clock over an entry, a shader the UI has no other use for, which the losses count.
- **Selling is production's.** A unit type's `sell` gives what its sale refunds and the time it takes; a player's sale of a building it controls refunds its train queue at once, marks it sold, sinks it as a site rises backwards over the time, then refunds and removes it with no death, as `BuildAssistant::sellObject` does; a sold building takes no order and stays a target. The import writes each structure's refund, its `RefundValue` or its cost times `SellPercentage`, and Zero Hour's 6 seconds. Refused: a sale as an action kind, which has no target, rank or cooldown; OpenRA's refund by the building's health, which Zero Hour does not take.
- **What the slice leaves.** The general's powers and sciences, guard, weapon switches, evacuations and the other commands of no engine order; places 15 to 18; a set's upgrade to another set; the queue's clock; the control bar's frame, its art and its minimap; a button's description; the sale's sounds and its rising money: each counted as a loss.

## Checks

- The client files' tests read an action's icon and key, a look's places, and the HUD file's grid and commands, and refuse an icon past its texture.
- The import's tests convert a mapped image to an icon of its atlas, a label's marked letter to a key, a command set's places to a look's places and its sell, stop, attack move and rally buttons to commands, and a structure's refund, and count each button and image they drop; the Zero Hour check pins the counts.
- Production's tests sell a building: its queue refunded at once, sold and sinking over its time, then its refund paid and it gone, and a sale of a site, of another player's building or of a sold one refused.
- The client's tests lay a look's buttons in the grid's places, press one by its key, show its tooltip, and dim one its player cannot pay.
- The Zero Hour check's play sells a building and pins its refund and its tick.
- In the client, a player of Tournament Desert trains and builds from the game's images, presses a button by its key, attack-moves and sets a rally from the panel, and sells a building.
