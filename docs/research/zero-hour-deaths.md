# Zero Hour's deaths

How Zero Hour shows a unit's and a building's death and a tracked vehicle's ground marks, and what the engine shows of them, read on 27 October 2026 from [GeneralsX](https://github.com/fbraz3/GeneralsX) (`SlowDeathBehavior.cpp`, `W3DTankDraw.cpp`, `W3DModelDraw.cpp`, `W3DTerrainTracks.cpp`, `GlobalData.cpp`), the install's `INIZH.big` (`Object/*.ini`, `ObjectCreationList.ini`, `FXList.ini`, `GameData.ini`), the import of the same day, and the engine's code.

## How the game shows a death

- **A slow death has three phases.** A `SlowDeathBehavior` keeps its object for `DestructionDelay` plus a draw within `DestructionDelayVariance`; it runs its `INITIAL` phase as the object dies, its `MIDPOINT` phase at a draw between 35 % and 65 % of that time, and its `FINAL` phase as it destroys the object. Each phase picks one of its `FX` lists, one of its `OCL` lists and one of its `Weapon`s at random, and plays each picked: an FX list, the objects an OCL creates, a weapon fired at the object's place. One slow death of an object's applicable ones is drawn by its `ProbabilityModifier`. `SinkDelay` and `SinkRate` sink the object into the ground, `FlingForce` throws it with physics (`SlowDeathBehavior::beginSlowDeath`, `doPhaseStuff`, `update`).
- **The install's slow deaths.** 1,493 `SlowDeathBehavior` modules: 1,210 `INITIAL`, 190 `FINAL` FX lines; 683 `INITIAL`, 24 `MIDPOINT`, 237 `FINAL` OCL lines; 1 `INITIAL`, 4 `MIDPOINT`, 43 `FINAL` weapons; 614 sink, 150 fling. The most named: `FX_DieByToxinGLA` 268, `OCL_ToxicInfantry` 143, `OCL_FlamingInfantry` 140, `FX_GIDieCrushed` 126, `FX_BuggyNewDeathExplosion` 89.
- **A tank burns, then explodes, and leaves a hulk and debris.** The Crusader has two slow deaths of equal weight for every death but `CRUSHED` and `SPLATTED`. The first: 500 to 600 ms, `FX_GenericTankDeathEffect` at its start, `OCL_GenericTankDeathEffect` at its midpoint, `FX_GenericTankDeathExplosion` and `OCL_CrusaderTurret` at its end. The second: 2,000 to 2,300 ms, `FX_CrusaderCatchFire` at its start, the same OCL and explosion at its end. `OCL_GenericTankDeathEffect` creates the object `DeadCrusaderHulk`, sent flying gently, and the debris models `GXMammoth_D01` to `D06` and more, sent flying; `OCL_CrusaderTurret` throws its turret and barrel, `AVLeopard_D2` and `D3`, upwards at 85° to 90° with a spin.
- **A hulk is a model that sinks.** `DeadCrusaderHulk` draws `AVLeopard_D1`, is `NO_COLLIDE` and `HULK`, falls and bounces with physics, lives 1.5 to 1.6 s, then its own slow death sinks it at 2 per second after 1.5 s and destroys it after 8 s.
- **A debris piece is a model thrown.** A `CreateDebris` names its models, offset, count, mass and disposition: `SEND_IT_FLYING` by an intensity, or `RANDOM_FORCE` by a force and a pitch range, and a spin rate.
- **A faction building explodes at once.** The War Factory's slow death is commented out; it has `DestroyDie`, which destroys it as it dies, `FXListDie` of `FX_StructureMediumDeath`, four particle systems, a sound and a subtle view shake, and two `CreateObjectDie`: `OCL_LargeStructureDebris`, power plant debris models thrown from its corners, and `OCL_AmericanRangerDebris05`.

- **A debris piece is a logic object of no gameplay.** A `CreateDebris` makes `GenericDebris`, `UNATTACKABLE`, drawn by `W3DDebrisDraw`, of one of its `ModelNames` at random, its `Mass`; it bounces with physics and dies resting on the ground, then sinks 1 per second after 1 s and goes at 2 s. `SEND_IT_FLYING` pushes it by a force of x and y within ±4 and z within 1 to 3 times its `DispositionIntensity`, `SEND_IT_UP` x and y within ±2 and z within 3 to 4 times it, `RANDOM_FORCE` by a magnitude and a pitch within its bounds; it spins at `SpinRate`, or π/32 times the intensity (`GenericObjectCreationNugget`, `ObjectCreationList.cpp`). `GameData.ini` sets `Gravity` to −64 per second squared.
- **The install's creation lists.** `ObjectCreationList.ini` holds 473 `CreateDebris`, 184 `CreateObject`, 46 `DeliverPayload`, 10 `ApplyRandomForce`, 4 `FireWeapon` and 1 `Attack`; dispositions 445 `SEND_IT_FLYING`, 81 `RANDOM_FORCE`, 39 `LIKE_EXISTING`, 28 `SEND_IT_OUT`, 21 `ON_GROUND_ALIGNED` and 1 `SEND_IT_UP`.
- **The import counts what deaths leave.** Of the import of 27 October 2026, by `EffectDropCause`: 0 objects whose slow death names a phase's second FX list, 194 whose slow death creates objects, 42 whose slow death fires a weapon, 87 whose draw throws tread dust and 192 whose draw lays track marks; 806 looks name a death.

- **Four kinds of slow death of their own.** 59 `JetSlowDeathBehavior`, 18 `HelicopterSlowDeathBehavior`, 6 `BattleBusSlowDeathBehavior` and 5 `NeutronMissileSlowDeathBehavior` modules extend the slow death. A jet's death plays `FXOnGroundDeath` and `OCLOnGroundDeath` when it dies on the ground; in the air it plays `FXInitialDeath` and `OCLInitialDeath`, rolls at `RollRate` and falls as its locomotor's lift drops to `FallHowFast` of gravity, plays `FXSecondary` and `OCLSecondary` after `DelaySecondaryFromInitialDeath`, `FXHitGround` and `OCLHitGround` as it hits the ground, and `FXFinalBlowUp` and `OCLFinalBlowUp` `DelayFinalBlowUpFromHitGround` after (`JetSlowDeathBehavior.cpp`). A helicopter's spirals down by its orbit's turn rate and speed and its own spin, throws its blade after a delay with `FXBlade` and `OCLBlade`, ejects its pilot by `OCLEjectPilot`, falls by `FallHowFast`, and plays `FXHitGround`, `OCLHitGround` and, `DelayFromGroundToFinalDeath` after, `FXFinalBlowUp`, `OCLFinalBlowUp` and its `FinalRubbleObject` (`HelicopterSlowDeathUpdate.cpp`). The helicopters are the Chinook, the Comanche and the Helix and their generals' copies; the jets the Raptor, the B-52, the B-3, the cargo plane and their copies; the battle buses the GLA's; the neutron missiles the nuke's, the cargo truck nuke's and the Baikonur rocket's.
- **What the import writes of them.** `IniDeath::module` takes `SlowDeathBehavior` and `FXListDie` alone, so a module of these four kinds keeps no unit, plays no effect and is counted as no loss. Of the import of 27 October 2026, the Comanche, the Helix and the Raptor are written, each of no `on_death`, so each despawns 9 ticks after its death; no battle bus or neutron missile is written.
- **A client cannot tell an end from a leave.** A dead unit's `Decay` is server state, never sent, and a unit that leaves a client's sight reaches it as a despawn, as one whose decay ended does (`bevy_replicon`'s visibility sends a despawn), so `MatchEffects::play_deaths` plays a death's end as the unit leaves sight too.

## How the game marks the ground

- **Tread dust.** A `W3DTankDraw` makes two particle systems on its drawable, `TreadDebrisLeft` and `TreadDebrisRight`, `TrackDebrisDirtLeft` and `TrackDebrisDirtRight` when the draw names none, and runs them while the object moves on the ground, its velocity and burst count scaled by its speed up to 1 m/s and more (`W3DTankDraw::doDrawModule`). 10 draws of the install name them.
- **A track's edge stands on the ground.** Each edge's two ends take the terrain's height at their point and stand 0.2 of a cell above it, a quarter of a unit on a bridge (`W3DTerrainTracks.cpp`).
- **Track marks.** A model draw's `TrackMarks` names a texture its vehicle lays on the terrain as it moves: an edge each 1.4 cells, of the width of a cell, up to 100 edges, the last 75 fading by distance and every edge over 300 s (`W3DModelDraw`, `W3DTerrainTracks.cpp`, `GlobalData.cpp`); `MaxTerrainTracks` = 100 vehicles and `MakeTrackMarks` = Yes in `GameData.ini`. 187 draws name one: `EXTireTrack.tga` 92, `EXTnkTrack.tga` 80, `EXTireTrack03.tga` 10, `EXTireTrack2.tga` 5.

## What the engine shows

- **The import reads a death's effect from `FXListDie` alone.** It names a look's `death` from the first applicable `FXListDie`'s `DeathFX`, and of a `SlowDeathBehavior` it reads only the delay of the most likely slow death, as a keep time (09-zero-hour.md, Deaths); a slow death's FX, OCL and weapons are neither converted nor counted as a loss. Of the client looks of 27 October 2026, 596 name a death; the Crusader, the Battlemaster, the Overlord, the Humvee and the Tomahawk name none.
- **A unit whose type despawns at death never shows it on a client.** A type of no `on_death`, as every faction building the import writes, takes `OnDeath::Despawn`, and `Dying::despawn_dead` despawns its dead unit at the end of the tick it died in, so the state the server sends after that tick holds no unit; a client never holds its `Dead`, and `MatchEffects::play_deaths`, which plays a death as a unit gains `Dead`, plays none. The client test of a death effect inserts `Dead` in the client's own world.
- **Ground marks.** Neither the tread dust nor the track marks are converted or counted as a loss.
- **A server frame runs 9 ticks at most.** `MAX_FRAME_TICKS` bounds a frame's catch-up, the rest of its time dropped (08-sessions.md, A burst after a stall runs 9 ticks at most), so a unit alive through the 9th tick after a tick is alive at the send of every frame that runs that tick.
- **`bevy_replicon` sends once a frame.** Its collect and send run in `PostUpdate` when `ServerTick` changed, from the world as it stands then (`ServerPlugin::build`, `bevy_replicon` 0.45.2); a send after each fixed tick of a frame needs its systems run in the fixed loop, which the crate does not offer.
- **The server sends once a frame.** `ServerSendPlugin` sends replication once a frame, after the fixed loop drains, so a frame that catches up several ticks sends the state after its last alone (`ServerSendPlugin::update_replication_tick`); a dead unit kept one tick more, which died in the first tick of such a frame, would still reach no client.

## Other engines

Read on 27 October 2026.

- **bevy_replicon.** Inserts, removals, despawns, events and messages reach a client in the order the server made them; a component inserted and removed before a send, the entity despawned with it, reaches no client, which receives the removal alone; a client may override a received despawn through `ReplicationRegistry::despawn` (`bevy_replicon` 0.45.2, crate documentation, Eventual consistency).
- **Netcode for Entities.** The server tells each client which ghosts to despawn; a client despawns an interpolated ghost once its interpolation tick reaches the despawn's tick, and a predicted one as soon as it reads the despawn ([GhostDespawnSystem](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode@1.5/api/Unity.NetCode.GhostDespawnSystem.html)); no documented pattern plays a death before a despawn.
- **Source.** An explosion or an impact is a temporary entity, sent once as an unreliable event and never updated, dropped past 32 in one update in multiplayer; entity networking carries what must arrive ([Temporary entity](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Temporary_entity)).
- **Bevy 0.20's decals.** A `ForwardDecal` is a quad that blends with what the camera's depth prepass holds, so its camera needs `DepthPrepass`, and it stretches at a steep view; a `ClusteredDecal` projects a texture onto every surface in its box with no prepass, through bindless textures, which some versions' documentation says macOS lacks ([Decal example](https://bevy.org/examples/3d-rendering/decal/), [ForwardDecal](https://doc.qu1x.dev/bevy_trackball/bevy/pbr/decal/struct.ForwardDecal.html)). The client's camera holds no depth prepass.
- **Spring.** Its ground decals, unit tracks among them, take the heightmap's own coordinates, and draw flat over a slope, which its tracker left unfixed ([Mantis 3033](https://springrts.com/mantis/view.php?id=3033), [forum](https://springrts.com/phpbb/viewtopic.php?p=469610)).
- **Halo: Reach.** Its replication has three kinds: state, eventually consistent; events, one-shot and unguaranteed, with their context; and control data, the inputs prediction reads, as summaries of David Aldridge's GDC 2011 talk give it; the talk itself was not read ([Edgegap](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration)).
- **Unreal.** A dying actor is torn off: the server sets `bTearOff`, which replicates to its clients as a final state and calls `TornOff` there, where the client plays its death, an animation or a ragdoll, on its own, and the server sends the actor no more ([Torn-off actors](https://docs.unrealengine.com/udk/Three/ReplicationTornOff.html), [AActor::TornOff](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/API/Runtime/Engine/GameFramework/AActor/TornOff/index.html)).
